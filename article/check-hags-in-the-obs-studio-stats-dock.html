Check HAGS in the OBS Studio Stats Dock

Hardware-Accelerated GPU Scheduling (HAGS) reduces latency and optimizes frame allocation between graphically demanding games and OBS Studio. While OBS does not feature an explicit on/off indicator labeled "HAGS" in its interface, you can verify that the scheduler is actively protecting your stream pipeline by analyzing key performance data in the Stats dock. This guide explains which metrics confirm HAGS is functioning properly and how to interpret them under heavy system loads.

Accessing the Stats Dock

Open OBS Studio, navigate to the top menu, click Docks, and select Stats. Keep this window visible on a secondary monitor or alongside a demanding 3D application to monitor real-time rendering behavior.

1. Average Time to Render Frame

The Average time to render frame metric tracks how long your GPU takes to composite a single canvas frame before sending it to the encoder.

  • Target Value: Typically between 1.0 ms and 5.0 ms (well below the 16.6 ms maximum required for a 60 FPS broadcast).
  • HAGS Confirmation: Under a 95% to 99% GPU gaming load, an active and effective GPU scheduler prevents the primary game from starving OBS of rendering compute. If HAGS is functioning alongside OBS's DirectX 11 priority hooks, this render time will remain stable and will not spike above the frame budget (16.6 ms for 60 FPS, 16.7 ms for 59.94 FPS, or 33.3 ms for 30 FPS).

2. Frames Missed Due to Rendering Lag

Directly below render times, the dock displays Frames missed due to rendering lag as both a total count and a percentage.

  • Target Value: 0.0% (or fewer than a dozen frames during scene transitions).
  • HAGS Confirmation: When HAGS is properly handling the scheduling queue in Windows 10/11, OBS retains its prioritized access to the GPU memory and execution pipelines. If you push your GPU to 100% capacity with an uncapped framerate game and the missed frames counter remains at 0.0%, HAGS and GPU prioritization are actively working. If HAGS is misconfigured or failing, this percentage will climb rapidly as the game deprives the compositor of execution cycles.

3. Frames Dropped Due to Encoding Lag

While primarily tied to the dedicated hardware encoder (such as NVENC, AMD AMF, or Intel QuickSync), Frames dropped due to encoding lag provides a secondary confirmation of proper scheduling.

  • HAGS Confirmation: HAGS bypasses traditional software-managed scheduling queues to feed frames directly to hardware components. When operational, the handoff from the OBS render pipeline to the hardware encoder remains uninterrupted, ensuring the encoder queue does not stall out even during sudden spikes in visual complexity.

How to Run the Verification Test

  1. Enable Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling in your Windows Display Settings and restart your PC.
  2. Launch OBS Studio as an Administrator (required for proper scheduling priority hooks).
  3. Open the Stats dock.
  4. Launch a GPU-intensive game and uncap its framerate to force 99–100% GPU utilization.
  5. Observe the Stats dock for 2 to 3 minutes during intense gameplay. If Frames missed due to rendering lag stays at 0.0% and the Average time to render frame remains well under 16 ms, hardware-accelerated scheduling is successfully managing your system's GPU resources.
