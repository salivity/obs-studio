Test OBS Stream Bandwidth Without Going Live

Testing your internet connection before broadcasting is crucial for ensuring a stable, lag-free stream. This guide explains how to natively test your stream bandwidth in OBS Studio using built-in features for Twitch and YouTube, allowing you to analyze your bitrate and connection stability without actually going live to your audience.

Method 1: Twitch Bandwidth Test Mode

If you stream to Twitch, OBS Studio has a built-in feature that allows you to send data to Twitch servers to test your connection without broadcasting to your channel.

Option A: Connected Account (Easiest)

  1. Open OBS Studio and go to Settings > Stream.
  2. Set your Service to Twitch and ensure your account is connected.
  3. Check the box labeled Enable Bandwidth Test Mode.
  4. Click Apply and then OK.
  5. Click Start Streaming on the main OBS interface. A warning will appear reminding you that you are in bandwidth test mode; click Yes to proceed.

Option B: Using a Stream Key

  1. Go to your Twitch Creator Dashboard > Settings > Stream and copy your Primary Stream Key.
  2. Open OBS Studio, go to Settings > Stream, and paste your key.
  3. Append ?bandwidthtest=true to the end of your stream key (e.g., live_12345678_abcdefg?bandwidthtest=true).
  4. Click Apply and then OK.
  5. Click Start Streaming.

Method 2: YouTube Private Stream Test

While YouTube does not have a dedicated "test mode" flag like Twitch, you can achieve the same result by streaming to a private destination.

  1. Go to the YouTube Creator Studio and click Go Live.
  2. Create a new stream and set the Visibility to Private or Unlisted.
  3. Copy the Stream Key from the YouTube dashboard.
  4. Open OBS Studio, go to Settings > Stream, set the service to YouTube, and paste the stream key.
  5. Click Apply and then OK.
  6. Click Start Streaming. Your data will send to YouTube, but no viewers will be notified, and your stream will not be public.

How to Analyze Your Test Results in OBS

Once you have started your test stream using one of the methods above, you need to monitor OBS to verify your connection quality.

  1. In OBS Studio, go to the top menu and select View > Docks > Stats.
  2. Look at the Dropped Frames (Network) section. If this percentage is above 0%, your bitrate is too high for your current upload speed.
  3. Observe the status bar in the bottom-right corner of OBS.
    • Green square: Your connection is stable.
    • Yellow/Red square: Your connection is struggling, and you should lower your video bitrate in Settings > Output.
