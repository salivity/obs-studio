OBS Multi-Camera Lip-Sync Issues Explained

Lip-sync errors when switching between multiple cameras in OBS Studio are primarily caused by hardware processing disparities, where each camera requires a different amount of time to process and deliver video frames to your computer. Because OBS Studio applies a single, global audio delay to a microphone input by default, matching your audio to one camera inevitably causes synchronization errors when switching to another camera with a different latency baseline.

Different Onboard Image Processing Pipelines

Every camera model processes visual information differently. When light hits an image sensor, the camera’s internal Image Signal Processor (ISP) must execute several computationally heavy tasks:

  • Demosaicing (converting raw sensor data into full color)
  • Noise reduction and sharpness filtering
  • Color grading and dynamic range correction
  • Compression and video encoding for the output port (HDMI, SDI, or USB)

A mirrorless or DSLR camera running advanced internal picture profiles and autofocus tracking often introduces between 80 to 200 milliseconds (ms) of internal processing latency. In contrast, dedicated webcams or industrial broadcast cameras may produce an output with as little as 20 to 50 ms of latency. When two different camera models are connected to OBS, their video feeds arrive at the capture cards at fundamentally different intervals.

Capture Device and Hardware Latency

Latency discrepancies are further amplified by the hardware capture path. If one camera is connected via a direct PCIe capture card and another is routed through a USB 3.0 capture dongle, the USB device will usually introduce additional bus packetization delay and frame buffering.

If one camera outputs at 4K 30fps while another outputs at 1080p 60fps, the capture devices and OBS Studio itself must spend varying amounts of time scaling and buffering each distinct stream, widening the latency gap between feeds.

The Problem with OBS Global Audio Sync

In OBS Studio, audio sources (such as a dedicated USB or XLR microphone) are typically handled as global audio devices. In the Advanced Audio Properties panel, users can set a "Sync Offset" in milliseconds to push the audio back so it aligns with the video.

However, this sync offset applies to the audio track as a whole, not per visual scene. If your primary camera has a 120 ms delay and your secondary camera has a 40 ms delay, configuring an audio offset of 120 ms will produce perfect lip-sync on the primary camera. The moment you cut to the secondary camera, the audio will arrive 80 ms after the speaker's lips move, producing an obvious lip-sync error.

How to Resolve Multi-Camera Lip-Sync Errors

To eliminate lip-sync discrepancies across multiple cameras in OBS Studio, apply one of the following methods:

  1. Normalize Video Latency with the Video Delay Filter: Determine which camera has the highest latency. On any faster cameras, right-click the video capture source in OBS, open Filters, and add a Video Delay (Async) filter. Add milliseconds to the faster cameras until all feeds arrive at the exact same delay. Once synchronized, set your global microphone offset to match that unified latency.
  2. Route Audio Through the Camera: Connect your microphone directly to the primary camera's microphone input (or route both into a hardware switcher). Because the audio is embedded into the camera's video stream before HDMI/SDI transmission, the hardware ISP delays both signals equally, maintaining lip-sync regardless of internal processing times.
  3. Use Dedicated Audio Sources Per Scene: Rather than using a global microphone, disable the mic in global settings and add it as an "Audio Capture Device" source inside individual scenes. By utilizing scene-specific audio inputs or plugins such as specialized audio delay filters on distinct sources, you can apply independent sync offsets tailored to the specific camera displayed in each scene.
