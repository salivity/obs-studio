Diagnose ISP Throttling vs Packet Loss in OBS Logs

Analyzing OBS Studio log files is the most reliable method for determining whether dropped frames stem from local network packet loss or deliberate Internet Service Provider (ISP) throttling. While both issues manifest as dropped frames due to network congestion, their behavioral signatures within OBS logs are distinctly different. By evaluating frame drop percentages, socket error messages, and bitrate behavior under controlled tests, you can accurately identify whether the bottleneck exists within your local hardware or upstream with your provider.

Accessing the Relevant OBS Log Data

To diagnose connection issues, you must inspect the session summary generated when a stream stops:

  1. Replicate the issue during a live broadcast or test stream.
  2. Stop the stream.
  3. Navigate to Help > Log Files > View Current Log (or upload the log via Upload Current Log for easier reading).
  4. Scroll to the bottom of the file to find the stream summary line, which looks similar to: Output 'adv_stream': Number of dropped frames due to insufficient bandwidth/connection stalls: XXXX (XX.X%)

Identifying Local Network Packet Loss

Local packet loss—caused by failing Ethernet cables, Wi-Fi interference, or congested home routers—generates erratic, non-linear patterns in OBS Studio.

  • Inconsistent Dropped Frame Rates: In the log, frame drops caused by local issues appear erratically. You will notice sudden bursts of drops followed by periods of perfect stability, regardless of the bitrate you set.
  • Socket Disconnections and Timeouts: Local hardware drops often cause physical interruption of the data stream. Logs will frequently display socket-level errors, such as: Write error: Connection reset by peer or Socket write failed: 10054 (Windows).
  • Failure Across Low Bitrates: If you lower your stream bitrate significantly (e.g., from 6,000 kbps to 2,000 kbps) and the log still reports intermittent dropped frames or high connection stall percentages, the issue is physical packet loss on the local network rather than a bandwidth restriction.

Identifying ISP Throttling

ISP throttling is characterized by artificial bandwidth ceilings, protocol shaping, or scheduled traffic management. The log displays consistent, predictable failure states rather than chaotic bursts.

  • The Bitrate Ceiling Effect: When an ISP throttles RTMP/RTMPS traffic, the stream remains stable until your output reaches the throttled limit. In your logs, a 4,000 kbps stream might show 0.0% dropped frames, whereas a 6,000 kbps stream will consistently show dropped frames roughly equivalent to the data exceeding the cap (e.g., sustained ~30% drops).
  • Buffer Congestion Without Socket Drops: Unlike local hardware failures that sever connections, throttling allows the TCP handshake to persist while slowing down transmission. The log will report high numbers of "insufficient bandwidth/connection stalls" as the internal OBS send buffer fills up and discards frames, but it will rarely display low-level network socket disconnect errors.
  • Time-of-Day Clustering: Comparing logs across different times will show clear scheduling. If logs recorded between 7:00 PM and 11:00 PM consistently report dropped frames at standard bitrates, while logs recorded during off-peak hours (e.g., morning or midday) show 0% dropped frames with the identical settings and local hardware, your ISP is managing evening network capacity.

Diagnostic Confirmation Step

To confirm throttling versus local loss, enable Dynamic Bitrate in OBS (Settings > Advanced > Network > Dynamically change bitrate to manage congestion):

  • If throttled: The log will show your bitrate dropping to a specific number and remaining flat at that lower threshold throughout the session.
  • If local packet loss: The log will show erratic, rapid oscillations in bitrate as the algorithm constantly tries and fails to find a stable connection baseline.
