ASMedia USB Mic Audio Clicks in OBS Studio

Capturing USB microphone audio through ASMedia USB host controllers in OBS Studio frequently results in audible clicks, pops, and micro-stutters. This issue is primarily caused by isochronous data transfer bottlenecks, driver-level DPC latency spikes, bandwidth sharing across motherboard PCIe lanes, and power management throttling. Understanding the technical conflicts between ASMedia hardware, Windows drivers, and OBS Studio's real-time audio processing pipeline helps isolate and resolve the root cause.

Isochronous Transfer Packet Loss

USB microphones rely on isochronous transfers to stream continuous, uncompressed digital audio in real time. Unlike bulk transfers (used for external drives), isochronous transfers do not retransmit dropped packets; missing data simply results in an immediate gap in the audio waveform, heard as a click or pop. ASMedia host controllers (such as the ASM1142, ASM2142, or ASM3142 series) frequently suffer from buffer underruns under continuous streaming loads, dropping audio packets before they reach OBS Studio's audio processing engine.

High DPC Latency and Driver Inefficiencies

Deferred Procedure Call (DPC) latency is critical for real-time audio. When a hardware driver takes too long to execute its tasks, it prevents the Windows audio subsystem and OBS from processing audio buffers within the allocated time window. ASMedia’s proprietary eXtensible Host Controller Interface (xHCI) drivers often exhibit high DPC latency spikes compared to native Intel or AMD chipset controllers. When DPC latency spikes, the audio buffer starves, generating an audible click.

PCIe Bus Contention and Multiplexing

Unlike native USB ports connected directly to the CPU or the primary motherboard chipset, ASMedia controllers are secondary, third-party chips added to expand port capacity. They typically communicate through secondary PCIe lanes routed through the motherboard chipset or shared with SATA controllers and PCIe x1 slots. If another device requests bus bandwidth—such as a webcam, capture card, or high-throughput drive—the ASMedia controller can experience momentary transit delays, interrupting the audio data stream.

Aggressive Power Management

Windows features like USB Selective Suspend and PCIe Active State Power Management (ASPM) attempt to conserve power by placing USB controllers and ports into lower-power states during perceived micro-idle periods. ASMedia chipsets do not always resume from low-power states fast enough to service real-time audio requests. This micro-throttling disrupts the steady polling interval required by high-resolution USB microphones.

Sample Rate and Buffer Inconsistencies in OBS

OBS Studio operates at a fixed internal audio sampling rate, typically 48 kHz. When an ASMedia controller introduces clock jitter or minor timing drift, the audio stream deviates slightly from OBS's internal clock. If the microphone's hardware sample rate in the Windows Sound Control Panel does not match OBS Studio exactly, OBS must resample the audio on the fly. Clock jitter from the ASMedia controller combined with resampling leads to buffer mismatches and repetitive audio clicking.

How to Mitigate the Issue

  • Move to Native USB Ports: Connect the microphone to native USB ports wired directly to the CPU or the primary chipset (usually located near the top of the motherboard rear I/O panel) rather than ports routed through ASMedia controllers.
  • Match Sample Rates: Ensure the microphone's default format in the Windows Sound Control Panel matches the sample rate selected in OBS Studio Settings > Audio (strictly 48 kHz across both).
  • Switch Controller Drivers: In Windows Device Manager, test switching the ASMedia controller from the manufacturer-provided driver to the generic Microsoft "USB xHCI Compliant Host Controller" driver, which often handles DPC latency more reliably.
  • Disable Power Throttling: Turn off "USB selective suspend setting" in Windows Advanced Power Options and uncheck "Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power" in the USB controller properties in Device Manager.
