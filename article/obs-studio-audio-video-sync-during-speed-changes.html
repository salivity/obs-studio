OBS Studio Audio Video Sync During Speed Changes

OBS Studio maintains synchronization between audio and video during dynamic speed changes through a combination of Presentation Time Stamps (PTS), audio resampling, and a centralized master clock. By scaling media timestamps to a modified timeline and dynamically adjusting audio sample buffers to match video frame intervals, OBS prevents drift, jitter, and buffer desynchronization during variable-speed playback.

When a media source alters its playback speed, the underlying decoder—typically handled via FFmpeg libraries—reads the container's native Presentation Time Stamps and scales them proportionally by the target speed factor. Instead of relying on real-time passage to govern playback, OBS anchors incoming frames and audio packets to this scaled PTS timeline. This ensures that both elements are mapped to the same relative temporal offset before entering the mixing pipeline.

Audio and video handle speed variance differently at the data level. Video speed changes are managed by altering the intervals at which frames are submitted to the OBS graphics subsystem; frames are displayed faster or held longer based on the modified PTS. Audio, however, cannot simply be played at irregular intervals without distorting pitch or causing buffer under-runs and over-runs. OBS utilizes audio processing filters (such as FFmpeg’s atempo or internal resamplers) to stretch or compress the audio waveform in real time. This process preserves the original pitch while matching the adjusted duration of the corresponding video frames.

To account for minor hardware discrepancies or processing latency during sudden speed shifts, OBS employs active drift correction. The software continuously calculates the differential between the audio subsystem's playback position and the video renderer’s presentation queue. If the audio drift exceeds a defined threshold, OBS makes micro-adjustments by slightly resampling audio chunks or dropping/duplicating video frames. This continuous calibration forces the media source to conform to OBS's global high-resolution nanosecond clock.

Once the media source's audio and video are aligned to the scaled timeline, they are passed into OBS's master render and audio output pipelines. The video is composited onto the canvas at the project's base framerate, and the audio is mixed down to the project's target sample rate (typically 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz). This ensures that dynamic speed changes within an individual media source remain completely isolated to that source and do not compromise the timing of the overall live stream or recording.
