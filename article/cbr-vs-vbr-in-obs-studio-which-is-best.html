CBR vs VBR in OBS Studio: Which is Best?

Choosing the right bitrate control method in OBS Studio is crucial for balancing video quality and file size. This article explains the core differences between Constant Bitrate (CBR) and Variable Bitrate (VBR), highlighting how each impacts your recordings, and provides clear recommendations on which setting to choose for your specific content creation needs.

What is Constant Bitrate (CBR)?

Constant Bitrate (CBR) transmits data at a fixed rate throughout the entire duration of your recording. Regardless of whether your screen is showing a static desktop or a fast-paced, high-action gaming sequence, the encoder will use the exact same amount of data per second.

  • Pros: Highly predictable file sizes and stable performance. Because the data rate never spikes, it is the industry standard for live streaming, as it prevents network congestion.
  • Cons: Highly inefficient for local recording. During simple scenes, CBR wastes storage space by using unnecessary data. During complex scenes, if the set bitrate is too low, the video will suffer from pixelation and artifacts.

What is Variable Bitrate (VBR)?

Variable Bitrate (VBR) dynamically adjusts the data rate based on the complexity of the visual content on screen. When there is little movement, the bitrate drops to save space. When intense motion or complex imagery occurs, the bitrate spikes to preserve visual quality.

  • Pros: Excellent space efficiency and superior video quality. VBR ensures that data is only used when it is actually needed, resulting in a better quality-to-file-size ratio.
  • Cons: Unpredictable final file sizes. Because the bitrate fluctuates, you cannot easily estimate how large a recording will be before you finish. It also requires slightly more processing power to encode.

Key Differences for OBS Recording

1. Quality Consistency

With CBR, video quality can fluctuate depending on the action on screen. If your bitrate is set to 6,000 Kbps, a static talking-head shot will look pristine, but a fast-motion game might look blurry. VBR prevents this by raising the bitrate during intense moments to keep the visual quality consistent.

2. Storage Efficiency

If you record a 60-minute video using CBR at 10,000 Kbps, the file size will be the same whether you recorded a blank screen or a rollercoaster ride. With VBR, the blank screen recording would result in a tiny file, while the rollercoaster ride would result in a larger, high-quality file.

3. CPU and GPU Load

CBR is easier on your computer's hardware because the encoder does not have to analyze the complexity of the frames to adjust the bitrate on the fly. VBR requires your hardware (CPU or GPU encoder) to perform real-time analysis, which can slightly increase system resource usage.

Recommendation: Which Should You Use in OBS?

For local recording, VBR (or its OBS alternatives like CQP and CRF) is the superior choice. It ensures your fast-paced gameplay or tutorials remain crisp without wasting gigabytes of storage space during quiet moments.

For live streaming, CBR is the mandatory choice. Streaming platforms like Twitch and YouTube require a steady, unfluctuating stream of data to prevent buffering for your viewers.
