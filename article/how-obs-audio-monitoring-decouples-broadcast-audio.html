How OBS Audio Monitoring Decouples Broadcast Audio

OBS Studio manages audio by isolating the local listen-back path from the final broadcast mix through a dedicated monitoring device pipeline. By configuring a distinct audio monitoring device, creators split incoming signals into two independent processing routes: one destined for the recording and streaming encoders, and another routed directly to local listening hardware. This separation prevents recursive feedback loops, enables independent volume adjustments, and allows creators to audition sources privately without broadcasting them to the audience.

Dual-Pipeline Audio Routing

At the core of OBS Studio's audio engine is a split-bus architecture. Every audio source added to a scene enters the internal mixer, where it can be routed along two separate paths: the Program/Output Bus and the Monitoring Bus.

The Program Bus delivers mixed audio to the active recording and streaming encoders based on track assignments (Tracks 1 through 6). The Monitoring Bus acts as an auxiliary send that routes audio exclusively to the hardware destination chosen under Settings > Audio > Advanced > Monitoring Device. Because this send bypasses the final stream mix, OBS can route signals to the creator's ears via a completely independent hardware driver.

Monitoring Modes in Advanced Audio Properties

The decoupling mechanism is controlled per-source via the Advanced Audio Properties window. Here, each input can be assigned one of three operational states:

  • Monitor Off: The source routes solely to the Program Bus. It is encoded to the stream or recording according to its track checkboxes, but no signal is sent to the monitoring hardware.
  • Monitor Only (Mute Output): The source is completely severed from the Program Bus and sent exclusively to the selected monitoring device. This mode is used for off-stream communication, director cues, or personal reference tracks that the audience must not hear.
  • Monitor and Output: The source is duplicated at the mixer stage. One clean copy continues down the Program Bus to the encoder, while an identical copy is tapped and routed directly to the monitoring device.

Preventing Desktop Audio Feedback Loops

A major technical benefit of this decoupled structure is the elimination of infinite audio feedback. In standard streaming setups, OBS often captures "Desktop Audio" as a primary sound source. If monitored audio were sent to that same desktop output, the software would capture its own monitor feed, re-broadcast it, and trigger a cascading echo loop.

By assigning the Audio Monitoring Device to a separate physical hardware output (such as a dedicated headphone jack, secondary soundcard, or virtual audio cable), the monitored signal exists entirely outside the desktop capture boundary. OBS outputs the listen-back feed directly to the listener’s ears without re-injecting that signal into the scene's capture sources.

Independent Gain and Volume Calibration

Decoupling the two feeds also ensures that physical listening levels do not compromise the broadcast mix. The faders in the OBS Audio Mixer control the volume of the signal sent to the stream and recording encoders.

Conversely, the loudness experienced through the monitoring feed can be adjusted via physical headphone volume controls or Windows sound settings on the target monitoring hardware. This allows the creator to increase or decrease personal monitoring volume to comfortable levels without altering the normalized decibel levels required for a clean, distortion-free stream output.
