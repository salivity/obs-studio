OBS Audio Monitoring vs Track Recording Explained

OBS Studio separates audio monitoring from track recording assignments in its Advanced Audio Properties matrix because local listening and destination encoding serve entirely different functional purposes. While audio monitoring controls what the creator hears in real time through their headphones, track assignments determine how audio is routed, mixed, and saved to the live stream or multitrack recording files. Decoupling these systems prevents audio feedback loops, enables flexible post-production workflows, and allows creators to independently customize what they hear versus what their audience hears.

Local Playback vs. Final Output Delivery

Audio monitoring is purely a local utility. When you set an audio source to "Monitor and Output" or "Monitor Only," OBS routes that sound directly to the monitoring device defined in your global settings (typically your headphones). This ensures you can verify audio fidelity, listen to scene transitions, or keep tabs on media playback.

In contrast, the numbered Tracks (1 through 6) in the matrix dictate where audio is routed for external delivery. Track 1 is commonly designated as the primary mix sent to a live broadcast on platforms like Twitch or YouTube, while Tracks 2 through 6 are typically reserved for multitrack recording. Because the end listener is completely different—the creator versus the audience—OBS manages these pipelines separately.

Prevention of Audio Echo and Feedback Loops

If monitoring and track assignments were locked together, audio feedback loops would be frequent and difficult to troubleshoot. For example, if a creator captures their default desktop audio and simultaneously monitors an alert through that same desktop device, a combined system would re-capture the monitored sound, creating an infinite echo.

By isolating monitoring from the track matrix, OBS allows you to send an audio source directly to a designated physical monitoring device without automatically re-injecting it into the stream or recording tracks.

Multitrack Post-Production Flexibility

Video editors require isolated audio stems to balance levels, remove unwanted noises, or eliminate background music during post-production. In OBS, the 1–6 matrix allows a creator to map:

  • Track 1: Full stereo master mix (Microphone + Game + Voice Chat)
  • Track 2: Isolated Microphone
  • Track 3: Isolated Game Sound
  • Track 4: Isolated Discord or Voice Chat

Because this matrix is separated from audio monitoring, your post-production track layout remains intact regardless of whether you want to hear a specific source in your headphones during the live session.

The Mix-Minus and "Secret Audio" Advantage

Separating these controls gives creators the ability to run a "mix-minus" setup directly within the software:

  • Monitor Only (Mute Output): Allows the streamer to hear audio that the stream cannot, such as a musical metronome, private producer cues, or audio cues from a capture card that is already being handled elsewhere.
  • Output Only (Monitor Off): Allows the audience to hear an audio source while sparing the streamer from hearing it. This is useful for desktop audio the creator can already hear natively outside OBS, preventing double-audio in the creator's ears.

By treating real-time monitoring and output routing as distinct operations, OBS Studio gives creators complete control over their acoustic environment without compromising the final recorded or broadcasted product.
