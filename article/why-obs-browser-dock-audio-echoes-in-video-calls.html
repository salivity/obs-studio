Why OBS Browser Dock Audio Echoes in Video Calls

Loading a web-based video conferencing room into an OBS Studio custom browser dock frequently results in disruptive audio echoing and feedback loops. This article explains the technical causes behind this issue, focusing on how OBS handles embedded browser audio, the interaction between system playback and desktop capture, and common configuration mistakes that create unintended audio loops.

Chromium Direct Audio Output

OBS Studio browser docks operate via the Chromium Embedded Framework (CEF). Unlike standard Browser Sources added directly to a scene, custom browser docks function as independent interface windows. By default, CEF routes audio directly to your operating system's default playback device, completely bypassing the OBS Audio Mixer. Because the audio is sent straight to your headphones or speakers without passing through OBS controls, you cannot natively mute, monitor, or manage the dock's audio levels within the software.

Desktop Audio Capture Loops

The primary cause of the echo is a classic feedback loop created by OBS Studio's "Desktop Audio" capture:

  1. The video conference inside the browser dock outputs incoming participant audio to your default Windows or macOS audio device.
  2. The global "Desktop Audio" capture in OBS records everything playing on that default device, including the conference call.
  3. If you route your OBS master output, virtual audio cable, or OBS Virtual Camera back into the conference call as your microphone input, the incoming audio is transmitted straight back into the meeting room.
  4. Participants hear their own voices returned to them after a short processing delay, creating an echo.

Running Dual Instances of the Call

Echoes also occur when users inadvertently load the conference call twice. Streamers often open the conference in a Custom Browser Dock to interact with controls or see participants, while simultaneously adding the same URL as a Browser Source in a scene to capture video.

Because both instances join the room independently:

  • Both instances receive and decode incoming audio streams.
  • The Browser Source outputs sound (either to the desktop or the OBS mixer).
  • The Browser Dock outputs sound directly to the system audio device.
  • The slight latency difference between the two renders creates a hollow, duplicated echo effect.

Lack of Native Acoustic Echo Cancellation (AEC)

Standard web browsers like Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge apply software-based Acoustic Echo Cancellation (AEC) and noise suppression when you join a meeting via their native interfaces. When a conference platform runs inside an OBS browser dock, it often lacks access to these native browser audio processing layers or hardware acceleration hooks. Without AEC active, any sound leaking from open-back headphones or desktop speakers into your open microphone is captured and rebroadcast into the room.

How to Eliminate the Echo

To prevent audio echoing when using browser docks for video conferencing:

  • Mute the Docked Tab: If the web platform provides an in-room speaker mute option, mute it inside the dock window so it produces no sound, and rely on an isolated capture method for stream audio.
  • Avoid Capturing Global Desktop Audio: Replace global Desktop Audio capture in OBS with the "Application Audio Output Capture" (available on Windows 10/11) to capture only the specific software programs you want your stream to hear.
  • Isolate Virtual Audio Cables: When sending OBS audio back into the meeting via a virtual microphone, ensure that Desktop Audio and the conference monitor are excluded from the virtual output track in OBS Advanced Audio Properties.
  • Do Not Double-Load Rooms: Use the dock strictly for monitoring/management without an active audio output, or capture the video via Window Capture from a standard, standalone browser instead of running both a dock and a Browser Source.
