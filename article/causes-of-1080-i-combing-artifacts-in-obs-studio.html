Causes of 1080i Combing Artifacts in OBS Studio

Interlace combing artifacts occur in OBS Studio when capturing 1080i signals because legacy interlaced video delivers alternating fields at different points in time, while modern streaming pipelines and computer monitors operate entirely in progressive frames. When OBS Studio receives an interlaced signal without active deinterlacing, it weaves two temporally offset fields into a single frame, resulting in jagged, horizontal "combing" or "feathering" lines along the edges of moving objects.

The Mechanics of 1080i Interlaced Video

A 1080i broadcast signal does not transmit full 1920x1080 video frames all at once. Instead, it divides each frame into two separate halves called "fields":

  • Field 1 (Odd lines): Contains lines 1, 3, 5, through 1079.
  • Field 2 (Even lines): Contains lines 2, 4, 6, through 1080.

In standard broadcast formats (such as 1080i at 59.94 Hz or 50 Hz), these fields are captured sequentially rather than simultaneously. In a 59.94i stream, a new field is captured every 1/59.94th of a second. As a result, Field 2 records visual data that occurred slightly later in time than Field 1.

The Progressive Processing Pipeline in OBS Studio

OBS Studio processes, renders, and outputs video exclusively as progressive frames (such as 1080p). Modern computer monitors, graphics cards, and web video players (like YouTube and Twitch) also strictly display progressive content, drawing the entire frame line-by-line from top to bottom at the same instant.

When a capture card sends a raw 1080i signal into OBS Studio, the software must interpret the incoming data. By default, unless configured otherwise, the software simply meshes (or "weaves") the incoming odd and even lines together to construct a single 1920x1080 frame.

Temporal Disparity: The Root Cause of Combing

When there is no movement in the video feed, the time difference between fields is unnoticeable because objects occupy the exact same coordinate space in both Field 1 and Field 2.

However, when an object moves horizontally or vertically across the screen:

  1. Field 1 captures the object at Position A.
  2. Roughly 16.7 milliseconds later (in 60 Hz systems), Field 2 captures the object at Position B.
  3. OBS displays Field 1 and Field 2 simultaneously on a progressive screen.

Because the object shifted between the capture of the alternating lines, every other horizontal line displays the object in an offset position. The visual consequence is the classic "comb" artifact—serrated, stair-stepped lines protruding from the edges of moving elements.

Capture Card Pass-Through Behavior

Capture cards typically handle 1080i in one of two ways:

  • Hardware Deinterlacing: Certain capture cards automatically deinterlace the signal on-board before passing a 1080p image to the operating system.
  • Direct Pass-Through: Most capture cards pass the raw, un-deinterlaced 1080i fields directly to the driver to reduce latency and processing overhead.

When using a direct pass-through capture card, OBS Studio receives the raw interlaced fields unmodified. If the user has not explicitly enabled a deinterlacing filter (such as Yadif 2x or Linear) in the capture card's source properties within OBS, the temporal mismatch remains visible in the preview window, canvas recordings, and outgoing stream.
