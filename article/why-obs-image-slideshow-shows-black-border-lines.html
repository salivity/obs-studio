Why OBS Image Slideshow Shows Black Border Lines

Thin black lines appearing along the borders of an Image Slideshow source during transitions in OBS Studio are typically caused by sub-pixel scaling, texture filtering artifacts, mismatched image aspect ratios, or alpha channel blending errors. This article explains the technical mechanics behind why these visual artifacts occur during transitions like fades and slides, along with how image processing in the OBS graphics pipeline triggers them.

Texture Filtering Bleed (Sampling Artifacts)

The primary cause of border lines during transitions is texture filtering. OBS Studio applies scaling algorithms—such as Bilinear, Bicubic, or Lanczos—to fit images onto your canvas. When an image is rendered, these interpolation filters sample neighboring pixels to smooth out edges.

During a transition (especially a fade or cross dissolve), the rendering engine samples the edge pixels against the adjacent empty canvas buffer, which defaults to fully transparent black rgba(0,0,0,0). If the texture coordinates do not clamp perfectly to the edges of the image texture, the filter samples this neighboring black data and blends it into the visible perimeter, creating a dark, semi-transparent fringe or thin line along the boundary.

Sub-Pixel Misalignment and Fractional Scaling

If your source images do not match the exact pixel dimensions of the OBS canvas or the slideshow's bounding box, OBS scales the images dynamically. This scaling often results in fractional pixel values (for example, rendering an image at a width of 1919.5 pixels).

Graphics hardware cannot render a fraction of a physical pixel, so it uses anti-aliasing to approximate the border. This anti-aliasing blurs the edge against the background buffer during active transition frames, manifesting as a flickering or static black line on the outer edges.

Inconsistent Aspect Ratios and Bounding Box Padding

The OBS Image Slideshow source utilizes a "Bounding Box" to maintain consistent dimensions across various files. If the images in your queue have varying dimensions or aspect ratios, OBS must pad the unused space to preserve the aspect ratio of each file.

During transitions, both the incoming and outgoing images are drawn simultaneously into the render target. If one image is slightly narrower or shorter than the other, the dark canvas under the smaller image becomes exposed, creating a brief black seam along the perimeter until the transition fully completes.

Premultiplied Alpha Processing

Transitions require compositing two separate image buffers together using alpha blending. If an image contains transparency, or if the transition shader blends non-premultiplied color values, color fringing can occur. When the software interpolates alpha values from 0% to 100%, incorrect handling of the color channels can inadvertently pull black levels into the edge pixels before full opacity is achieved.

How to Prevent Transition Border Lines

  • Standardize Image Dimensions: Crop or resize all images in photo editing software to the exact native resolution of your canvas (e.g., 1920x1080) before importing them into OBS.
  • Adjust Bounding Box Settings: In the Image Slideshow properties, set the Bounding Size/Aspect Ratio to match your output resolution, avoiding dynamic auto-scaling.
  • Change the Scale Filtering: Right-click the slideshow source, navigate to Scale Filtering, and test different modes. Switching to Point eliminates edge-sampling bleed completely, though it may make downscaled images look sharper or more pixelated.
