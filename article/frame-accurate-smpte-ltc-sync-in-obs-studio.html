Frame-Accurate SMPTE LTC Sync in OBS Studio

This article explains how OBS Studio ingests, decodes, and synchronizes external SMPTE Linear Timecode (LTC) audio signals to achieve frame-accurate multi-source alignment. By translating high-frequency audio waveforms into precise time values, mapping them to internal video frames, and compensating for hardware drift, OBS Studio ensures that live streams and isolated local recordings remain locked to a single master clock reference.

LTC Signal Ingestion and Demodulation

SMPTE LTC is transmitted as an audible analog signal encoded using Biphase Mark Code (BMC). In this format, binary zeros have a single transition at the start of a bit period, while binary ones contain an additional mid-bit transition.

When an external timecode generator (such as a Tentacle Sync or Ambient NanoLockit) feeds this signal into an audio interface connected to OBS Studio, the software captures it as a standard PCM audio stream. An internal decoder or specialized plugin continuously monitors this audio input, detecting voltage zero-crossings to extract the raw binary stream at 80 bits per frame.

Identifying Frame Boundaries via Sync Words

Each 80-bit LTC frame contains data representing hours, minutes, seconds, frame numbers, user bits, and drop-frame flags. Crucially, the final 16 bits of every LTC frame consist of a standardized "sync word" (0011 1111 1111 1101).

The decoder identifies this distinct bit sequence to determine:

  • The exact end of the current frame and the start of the next.
  • The playback or transmission direction (forward or reverse).
  • The precise sub-millisecond edge where the timecode frame transition occurs.

By pinning this edge to the incoming audio sample buffer, the system knows the exact timecode value down to the specific audio sample.

Internal Presentation Timestamp (PTS) Mapping

OBS Studio processes media using an internal pipeline governed by high-resolution system timestamps (Presentation Timestamps, or PTS). Under normal circumstances, video capture cards assign timestamps to frames using the operating system's monotonic clock.

When LTC synchronization is engaged, the decoded LTC time replaces or overrides the default capture timestamps:

  1. The audio thread decodes the LTC frame and records the exact system clock tick at which the 16-bit sync word concluded.
  2. The incoming video frames from capture devices (such as HDMI or SDI capture cards) arrive in the video pipeline with their own hardware capture timestamps.
  3. The synchronization engine matches the video frame boundary to the closest LTC frame boundary, effectively stamping the decoded SMPTE metadata directly onto the video frame object within memory.

Buffer Alignment and Latency Management

Video capture pipelines typically introduce more processing and buffering latency than analog audio lines. To maintain frame accuracy, OBS Studio utilizes circular memory buffers for all active sources.

When a video frame arrives late due to hardware capture delays, the software does not discard the timing metadata. Instead, it reads the correlated LTC time assigned to that frame and holds the frame in the buffer until the playback or render cursor reaches the corresponding timestamp. This aligns visual frames and discrete audio samples before they hit the compositor or recording muxer.

Clock Drift and Resampling Compensation

Frame drift occurs when the audio interface digitizing the LTC signal and the video capture card operate on independent quartz crystal oscillators. Over time, slight variations in hardware clocks cause audio samples and video frames to slip out of phase.

OBS Studio resolves this through timing drift correction:

  • Audio Resampling: If the incoming LTC audio sample rate drifts relative to the system render loop, the audio engine resamples the audio stream dynamically, inserting or removing fractional samples to keep the timecode edge locked to the video frame rate.
  • Timestamp Tracking: Rather than relying on a continuous count of audio samples, the timecode decoder constantly re-anchors itself to the absolute time values parsed from the incoming 80-bit words, preventing cumulative timing errors over long recording sessions.

Recording Container Metadata Injection

To preserve frame accuracy for post-production workflows, OBS Studio writes the synchronized SMPTE timecode directly into the recorded media file containers (such as MP4, MOV, or MKV).

Rather than merely displaying the time as a visual overlay, the muxer writes a dedicated timecode track (such as the tmcd track in QuickTime/MOV containers) or embeds Supplemental Enhancement Information (SEI) timing metadata into H.264/H.265 video streams. Non-linear editing systems (NLEs) read this embedded track to instantly align multi-camera footage captured through separate OBS instances or external hardware recorders down to the exact frame.
