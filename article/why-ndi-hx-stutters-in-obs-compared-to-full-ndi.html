Why NDI HX Stutters in OBS Compared to Full NDI

While it seems counterintuitive that low-bandwidth NDI|HX streams would stutter more than high-bandwidth, uncompressed Full NDI feeds, the issue stems from video compression architecture rather than network throughput. This article explains the fundamental differences between how OBS Studio and the NDI runtime process Full NDI (SpeedHQ) versus NDI|HX (H.264/HEVC), highlighting decoding bottlenecks, timing differences, and network packet behaviors that cause playback stuttering.

Hardware Decoding Overhead

Full NDI uses a lightweight intra-frame codec known as SpeedHQ. Because every frame is an independent image, decoding requires minimal computational power, allowing the CPU to decompress multi-gigabit streams with negligible latency.

In contrast, NDI|HX (High Efficiency) uses inter-frame compression algorithms like H.264 or HEVC to drastically reduce bandwidth. These codecs rely on complex compression that requires substantial decoding power. When OBS Studio ingests multiple NDI|HX streams, the system must decode these streams in real time using dedicated hardware decoders (such as Nvidia NVDEC or Intel QuickSync) or CPU software decoding. If the hardware decoder is saturated by simultaneous encoding/decoding tasks, or if OBS defaults to CPU decoding, the processing delay manifests as micro-stutters and dropped frames.

Inter-Frame Dependencies and Packet Loss

The structural difference between intra-frame and inter-frame data streams greatly impacts playback stability:

  • Full NDI (Intra-frame): If a network packet drops, the artifact is isolated to a fraction of a single frame. The decoder immediately recovers on the very next frame without stalling playback.
  • NDI|HX (Inter-frame / GOP): NDI|HX relies on a Group of Pictures (GOP) structure consisting of keyframes (I-frames) followed by predictive frames (P-frames and B-frames). If a single packet containing keyframe data is delayed or dropped, the decoder cannot render subsequent predictive frames. The OBS stream will freeze or stutter until the next complete I-frame arrives.

Timestamp Jitter and Buffer Starvation

OBS Studio processes audio and video through an internal synchronization clock that expects steady frame pacing. High-bandwidth Full NDI delivers frames almost linearly across the network. NDI|HX, due to its complex compression, often transmits packets in uneven bursts depending on the visual complexity of the scene.

This bursty transmission introduces packet arrival jitter. If the receiving NDI runtime does not have sufficient buffer depth configured inside OBS, the decoder experiences momentary buffer starvation. Because OBS prioritizes real-time presentation over latency buffering, it drops late-arriving frames rather than delaying the canvas rendering, causing noticeable visual stutter.

Network Switch Processing

High-bandwidth Full NDI relies on continuous, high-volume throughput that modern gigabit switches handle effortlessly via standard ASIC switching fabrics. NDI|HX devices, frequently deployed over Wi-Fi or unmanaged consumer switches, can suffer from packet pacing issues. Because NDI|HX packets are smaller and more compressed, any micro-burst congestion or aggressive Quality of Service (QoS) throttling on the local network disproportionately delays time-sensitive predictive video packets, leading directly to rendering hitches inside OBS.
