Why OBS Studio Converts Audio to 32-Bit Planar Float

OBS Studio standardizes all incoming audio into a 32-bit planar floating-point format to ensure maximum processing efficiency, prevent digital clipping, and simplify audio filtering. By converting disparate audio formats from microphones, capture cards, and desktop applications into a unified internal representation upon ingestion, OBS eliminates the risk of distortion during multi-track mixing while leveraging modern CPU architecture for high-speed digital signal processing.

Virtually Infinite Headroom and Anti-Clipping

When multiple audio sources are mixed together—such as a game, background music, and a microphone—their digital waveforms are combined through addition. In standard integer formats (like 16-bit or 24-bit PCM), summing signals that exceed 0 dBFS causes immediate, irreversible digital clipping and harsh distortion.

A 32-bit floating-point format represents audio samples using values where 1.0 typically corresponds to 0 dBFS, but the dynamic range extends far beyond this threshold (over 1,500 dB). This provides virtually limitless headroom. Signals can exceed 0 dBFS during intermediate mixing stages without losing peak data. Creators can apply compressors, limiters, or gain reductions later in the audio chain to bring the final master back within standard ranges cleanly before final encoding.

Efficient Digital Signal Processing (DSP)

Most digital audio processing algorithms—including equalizers, compressors, noise gates, and VST plugins—are fundamentally built on floating-point arithmetic. If OBS maintained integer audio natively, the audio engine would constantly convert samples back and forth between integer and floating-point representations for every applied filter.

Converting to float at the ingestion stage establishes a uniform standard across the entire pipeline. Once converted, audio filters can process the raw data directly, preserving precision, preventing round-off quantization errors, and minimizing CPU overhead.

SIMD Acceleration via Planar Channel Layout

Audio is conventionally captured in an interleaved format, where channels alternate sample by sample (e.g., Left, Right, Left, Right). OBS explicitly converts this to a planar format, storing entire blocks of Left channel samples contiguously, followed by Right channel samples.

Modern processors use SIMD (Single Instruction, Multiple Data) architectures, such as AVX, SSE, or ARM NEON, to perform the same operation on multiple data points simultaneously. A planar layout allows the CPU to load contiguous blocks of a single channel's audio directly into vector registers without requiring costly memory de-interleaving. This architectural alignment accelerates volume scaling, panning, filtering, and mixing operations across all channels.

Format Normalization Across Mixed Hardware

OBS Studio frequently ingests audio from wildly divergent devices:

  • Webcams transmitting 16-bit 44.1 kHz interleaved audio
  • USB microphones providing 24-bit 48 kHz audio
  • Virtual cables or desktop captures delivering 32-bit floating-point streams

Managing distinct mixing math for every combination of sample format would introduce extreme software complexity and latency. Normalizing all incoming streams into 32-bit planar float ensures the internal mixing engine only needs to support a single, highly optimized code path. The audio remains in this format through all intermediate routing and filtering, undergoing only one final down-conversion to the target format (such as 16-bit AAC or Opus) at the encoding stage.
