NDI QoS Settings for Managed Enterprise Switches

Deploying high-volume NDI (Network Device Interface) streams into OBS Studio requires precise Quality of Service (QoS) configurations on managed enterprise switches to eliminate dropped frames, jitter, and packet loss. High-bandwidth NDI relies heavily on consistent throughput and low latency across the local area network. This guide outlines the recommended Layer 2 (802.1p CoS) and Layer 3 (DSCP) traffic classification mappings, egress queue scheduling methods, buffer management strategies, and supporting network policies necessary to ensure reliable NDI delivery to an OBS Studio production workstation.

Traffic Classification and Prioritization Values

Enterprise switches must be configured to trust incoming QoS tags or actively classify NDI packets at ingress switch ports connected to NDI sources (such as PTZ cameras, encoders, and media servers).

  • Network Control & Discovery (mDNS):
    • DSCP Value: CS6 (DSCP 48)
    • 802.1p (CoS): Priority 6
    • Purpose: Ensures device discovery and network synchronization packets bypass congested data queues.
  • NDI Audio and Precision Timing:
    • DSCP Value: EF (Expedited Forwarding, DSCP 46)
    • 802.1p (CoS): Priority 5
    • Purpose: Keeps uncompressed audio packets and any timing-critical data ahead of the heavier video payload to avoid lip-sync drift.
  • NDI Video Payload:
    • DSCP Value: AF41 (Assured Forwarding 41, DSCP 34)
    • 802.1p (CoS): Priority 4 or 5 (depending on switch architecture)
    • Purpose: Designates the heavy, high-bitrate video streams as mission-critical, interactive video traffic.
  • General IT / Office Traffic:
    • DSCP Value: Best Effort (DSCP 0)
    • 802.1p (CoS): Priority 0
    • Purpose: Drops or delays non-essential background traffic during network congestion rather than impacting live media.

Egress Queue Scheduling

Proper egress scheduling ensures that prioritized packets leave switch ports in the correct order without entirely starving lower-priority operational traffic.

  • Strict Priority (SP) for Timing and Control: Assign the highest hardware queue (typically Queue 7 or 8) to Strict Priority. Map network control (CS6) and clocking traffic to this queue to ensure immediate egress.
  • Weighted Fair Queuing (WFQ) or Weighted Round Robin (WRR) for Video: Map NDI video (AF41) and audio (EF) to high-priority weighted queues (such as Queue 5 and Queue 6).
  • Bandwidth Allocation: Allocate at least 60% to 75% of the total interface bandwidth to the NDI video queue. This prevents starvation of the OBS Studio ingest link while maintaining a dedicated path for media frames.

Buffer Management and Flow Control

High-volume NDI streams generate microbursts that can overwhelm standard switch port memory.

  • Disable 802.3x Flow Control: Switch-wide flow control should be disabled on all ports handling NDI traffic. Flow control sends PAUSE frames during buffer saturation, which creates artificial latency, head-of-line blocking, and bursty frame delivery that can cause OBS Studio's NDI plugin to drop frames.
  • Packet Buffers and Shared Memory: Configure ports to utilize dynamic or shared packet buffering instead of strict static buffer allocations. NDI bursts require switches to dynamically allocate deeper buffer space on the egress port feeding the OBS Studio workstation.

Supporting Network Configuration Settings

In addition to QoS classification and queuing, these adjacent switch configurations are required for stable NDI routing to OBS Studio:

  • Enable IGMP Snooping and Querier: If running NDI Multicast or relying on mDNS discovery across complex network segments, enable IGMP Snooping and ensure an active IGMP Querier is configured on the media VLAN to prevent switch ports from being flooded with unwanted multicast traffic.
  • Trust Mode Configuration: Ensure ingress switch ports connected to NDI sources and the OBS Studio receiver are explicitly configured with trust dscp or trust cos so that tags are preserved across switch hops rather than being remarketed to DSCP 0.
  • Dedicated Media VLAN: Segregate all NDI sources and the OBS ingest machine onto a dedicated broadcast domain/VLAN to isolate high-throughput broadcast video from broadcast/multicast noise generated by general office infrastructure.
