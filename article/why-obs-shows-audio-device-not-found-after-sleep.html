Why OBS Shows Audio Device Not Found After Sleep

Waking a streaming PC from system sleep with OBS Studio actively running often triggers an "Audio device not found" warning in the application log. This warning occurs due to a timing mismatch between how quickly the operating system resumes background software processes and how long hardware audio drivers take to reinitialize. Understanding the root causes—such as hardware power states, USB bus re-enumeration, and monitor handshakes—explains why this error happens and whether it impacts your broadcast.

Hardware Re-initialization Latency

When a computer transitions from a low-power sleep state (such as ACPI S3 or Modern Standby) to fully awake (S0), the CPU resumes software execution almost instantly. In contrast, physical hardware requires time to power back up and re-establish communication with the operating system.

OBS Studio resumes running immediately upon wake and queries the Windows Audio service (WASAPI) to verify its configured capture and monitoring devices. If an external sound card, USB interface, or microphone takes even a fraction of a second longer to initialize than the software, the Windows Audio Endpoint Builder will report that the hardware is missing, causing OBS to generate the log warning.

USB Bus Enumeration and Power Management

USB audio devices are especially prone to this log warning because of how the operating system manages USB root hubs during sleep. Features such as "USB Selective Suspend" cut power to USB ports to save energy.

Upon waking, the host controller must supply power, negotiate connection speeds, enumerate connected peripherals, and reload the device drivers. If OBS attempts to poll an audio device during this multi-step enumeration process, the device will technically not exist in the active audio registry at that exact millisecond.

DisplayPort and HDMI Audio Handshakes

If you capture or monitor audio through a display connection—such as an external capture card, a television, or a gaming monitor connected via HDMI or DisplayPort—the device relies on an Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) handshake.

Display connections typically take several seconds to establish visual and audio links when waking from sleep. Because the audio transport layer is tied directly to the video signal, the operating system disables the audio endpoint completely until the display handshake concludes. OBS encounters an inactive endpoint during this window and logs the missing device.

System Impact and Next Steps

In the majority of setups, this warning is entirely cosmetic. Once the drivers complete their initialization, Windows reports the endpoint as active, and OBS Studio automatically re-binds to the audio stream.

However, if your audio fails to restore after waking, you can mitigate the issue by preventing the streaming PC from entering system sleep, disabling USB Selective Suspend in your Windows power plan, or restarting OBS Studio following a system wake event.
