OBS Studio Internal Audio Bit Depth Explained

This article examines the internal audio processing bit depth used by the OBS Studio audio mixer before encoding and final output. It explains the specific format OBS utilizes to handle audio streams, the journey of an audio signal through its processing chain, and the technical advantages this architecture provides for real-time sound quality and dynamic range management.

The Internal Processing Bit Depth: 32-Bit Float

OBS Studio handles all internal audio mixing and filter processing using 32-bit floating-point (single-precision IEEE 754 float) audio architecture.

Regardless of whether an incoming audio device is sending a 16-bit or 24-bit integer signal, OBS Studio immediately converts the incoming sample data to 32-bit float upon entering the software’s core audio pipeline (libobs).

How the OBS Studio Audio Pipeline Works

  1. Audio Ingestion: Physical capture devices (microphones, capture cards, desktop audio) typically deliver digital audio in 16-bit or 24-bit fixed-point integer PCM.
  2. Internal Conversion: Once ingested, the audio samples are unpacked and converted to 32-bit floating-point format.
  3. Filtering and DSP: Native OBS audio filters (such as gain, limiters, compressors, noise gates, and third-party VST plugins) process the signal entirely in the 32-bit float domain.
  4. Summing and Mixing: Individual sources are combined on the mixer tracks while maintaining the 32-bit float representation.
  5. Output Conversion and Encoding: Before writing to disk or sending the stream to a platform, OBS converts the mixed 32-bit float signal to the format expected by the chosen audio encoder. Most streaming and recording codecs (such as AAC or Opus) accept either 16-bit integer PCM or 32-bit float, ultimately quantizing and compressing the stream to standard delivery bitrates.

Why 32-Bit Float Matters

The primary technical benefit of using a 32-bit floating-point mixer is virtually unlimited dynamic range within the software.

In traditional 16-bit or 24-bit fixed-point processing, signals that exceed 0 dBFS (decibels relative to full scale) result in immediate digital clipping and distortion. In contrast, 32-bit float provides over 1,500 dB of dynamic range. This means:

  • Internal Headroom: If an audio filter or a loud source causes the signal to spike well above 0 dBFS within the mixer, the audio data is not clipped internally. As long as a limiter, compressor, or volume fader reduces the final output signal below 0 dBFS before it reaches the encoder, no digital clipping or distortion occurs.
  • Low Noise Floor: Repetitive mathematical operations across multiple filters introduce minimal rounding errors, preventing accumulated quantization noise from degrading the sound quality.
