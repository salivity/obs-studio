Why OBS Stutters With Mismatched Frame Rates

Micro-stuttering in OBS Studio recordings commonly occurs when capturing or rendering at a frame rate that does not evenly divide into the refresh rate of your monitor, such as recording at 50 frames per second (fps) on a 60 Hertz (Hz) display. This article explains the technical mechanics behind this issue, including irregular frame delivery, temporal aliasing, and display-timing conflicts, while detailing how aligning these variables restores smooth video playback.

The Problem of Mathematical Asymmetry

A standard 60Hz monitor refreshes its screen once every 16.67 milliseconds. Conversely, a 50fps video stream requires a new frame every 20 milliseconds. Because 50 does not divide evenly into 60, OBS Studio cannot synchronize its frame capture cycle with the display's hardware refresh cycle.

When OBS attempts to sample a 60Hz screen at 50fps, it must discard 10 incoming screen frames every second. Because 10 dropped frames spread across 60 cycles cannot be distributed symmetrically, OBS captures an irregular cadence:

  • Four consecutive frames are captured normally.
  • The fifth frame is dropped to compensate for the timing drift.
  • The cycle repeats unevenly across every second of recorded footage.

This uneven cadence disrupts the continuous motion flow, producing a rhythmic, repeating hitch known as micro-stutter or judder.

Capture Pipeline and Compositor Conflicts

The operating system's display pipeline compounds this timing discrepancy. Under Windows, the Desktop Window Manager (DWM) enforces vertical synchronization (VSync) based on the primary monitor's refresh rate (60Hz).

When OBS captures a window, desktop, or game, it queries the graphics pipeline for new frames. If a game runs at 60fps locked to a 60Hz monitor, each game frame is drawn every 16.67ms. Because the OBS render thread is configured to generate output at 20ms intervals (50fps), the OBS capture hook will occasionally poll the frame buffer right before a new frame is rendered or immediately after, capturing some game frames twice and completely skipping others. This results in erratic frame pacing baked directly into the recorded video file.

Playback-Side Judder

Even if OBS manages to encode a mathematically consistent 50fps file, playing that file back on a 60Hz monitor introduces a secondary stutter known as playback judder.

To display 50fps content on a 60Hz panel, the media player and GPU must stretch 50 frames across 60 refresh cycles. To fill the 10-frame deficit, the display holds certain frames on screen for two refresh cycles (33.33ms) instead of one (16.67ms). The human eye easily detects this periodic hold time, perceiving smooth motion suddenly hesitating multiple times per second.

How to Eliminate the Stutter

To resolve micro-stuttering caused by mismatched frame rates, eliminate the mathematical discrepancy between your source, recording settings, and playback hardware:

  • Match OBS to the Display: Set OBS Studio's output frame rate to 60fps in Settings > Video > Common FPS Values. This establishes a direct 1:1 relationship between monitor refresh intervals and recorded frames.
  • Match the Display to OBS: If 50fps is mandatory (such as for European PAL broadcasting compliance), change your monitor's refresh rate in the Windows Display Settings to 50Hz or an exact multiple, such as 100Hz.
  • Cap In-Game Frame Rates: If recording at 50fps on a 50Hz display, ensure your software or game is strictly capped at 50fps to prevent the graphics engine from rendering out-of-sync frames.
