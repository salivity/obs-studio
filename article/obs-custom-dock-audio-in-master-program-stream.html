OBS Custom Dock Audio in Master Program Stream

Routing a custom dock audio feed into the master program stream in OBS Studio shifts the signal from an isolated Chromium Embedded Framework (CEF) process into the native OBS audio mixing pipeline. This article examines how OBS Studio's internal audio mixer state changes during this process, detailing track bus allocation, sample rate conversion, clock synchronization, and the activation of filter chains across the master output.

Signal Ingestion and Mixer Bus Allocation

Custom browser docks in OBS Studio are rendered using an off-screen CEF instance. By default, dock audio plays through the operating system's default audio output device without interacting with the internal mixer.

When this feed is routed into the master program stream—either via a virtual audio device, desktop capture, or an internal bridge—the mixer registers the feed as an active input node in audio-io.c. The internal mixer dynamically creates a state entry for this source, binding it to the 6-track output matrix. Depending on the configuration in Advanced Audio Properties, the audio mixer enables routing bits for designated output tracks (Tracks 1 through 6), directing the uncompressed PCM stream into the corresponding track encoders.

Resampling and Buffer Synchronization

OBS Studio operates its internal audio engine at a fixed sample rate (typically 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz, defined in Audio Settings). Web audio generated from CEF docks often fluctuates in packet delivery or may operate at a different internal rate.

Upon entering the mixer state:

  1. Resampling: The audio engine passes the incoming stream through an internal resampler (using speex or native linear interpolation) to match the master engine's sample rate.
  2. Buffer Alignment: Audio samples are buffered into the mixer's circular queue. The mixer calculates presentation timestamps (PTS) to align the dock audio with the master video rendering clock, preventing audio drift during live encoding or recording.

Volume Scaling and Filter Pipeline Execution

Once ingested into the mixer state, the dock's raw audio data is subject to the per-source processing chain:

  • Fader Gain: The internal volume level converts logarithmic user fader settings to linear floating-point multipliers applied directly to the PCM buffer.
  • Filter Stack: If filters (such as Compressors, Noise Suppression, or VST plugins) are applied to the input container, the mixer runs the buffer through each filter sequentially before passing the data to the summing bus.
  • Peak Metering: The mixer state updates the volume meter telemetry in real time, calculating peak and RMS values that are dispatched to the UI thread for visual feedback.

Summing and Monitoring State Changes

After per-source processing, the mixer sums the dock audio into the master mix:

  • Master Downmixing/Panning: If the dock provides stereo audio and the master stream is configured for multichannel or mono, the internal mixer downmixes or maps the channels according to the channel layout state.
  • Audio Monitoring: If the source's monitoring state is set to "Monitor and Output" or "Monitor Only," the mixer forks the signal into the secondary monitoring bus. If the monitoring device matches the device capturing the custom dock, an internal feedback loop will occur within the mixer state, resulting in audio clipping and digital distortion on the master program stream.
