OBS Color Space Conversion for Camera Log Profiles

OBS Studio manages color space conversion for cinema camera log profiles by integrating modern color pipelines, source-level configuration, and 3D lookup table (LUT) processing. While OBS 28 and later introduced high-dynamic-range (HDR) and wide-color-gamut (WCG) infrastructure, it does not include an automated, built-in camera-profile transform engine like ACES or OpenColorIO. Consequently, handling log profiles requires understanding how OBS interprets hardware capture inputs, how it maps broad dynamic range values into standard internal spaces, and how software LUT filters normalize flat log gammas for broadcast and streaming.

The Internal Color Architecture of OBS Studio

Modern versions of OBS Studio utilize an internal color management pipeline capable of processing both Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) and High Dynamic Range (HDR) workflows. OBS supports working color spaces such as Rec. 709, sRGB, and Rec. 2100 (using either PQ or HLG transfer characteristics).

When a video frame enters the rendering pipeline, OBS transforms the ingested pixel data into linear RGB or a standardized compositing space, blends layers according to scene hierarchies, and then encodes the final composite into the designated output color space.

Ingesting Log Data Through Video Capture Devices

Cinema cameras outputting log gammas (such as Sony S-Log3, Canon C-Log, ARRI LogC, or Panasonic V-Log) transfer video over HDMI or SDI through a capture card. Most capture cards transmit the video stream as uncompressed YUV or RGB frames:

  1. EDID and Signal Handshake: Cinema outputs often transmit the log curve wrapped inside an ordinary video signal payload. Because capture hardware typically negotiates standard dynamic range parameters, the capture card treats the flat log signal as standard video data.
  2. Device Properties Configuration: Within the properties of an OBS "Video Capture Device" source, users manually designate the Color Space (e.g., Rec. 709 or Rec. 2100) and Color Range (Partial/Limited or Full).
  3. Hardware Level Quantization: If a camera outputs a full-range log signal, setting the capture source to "Full" prevents data clipping at standard black (16) and white (235) broadcast boundaries, retaining shadow and highlight details recorded by the sensor.

Tone Mapping and Log Conversion via LUT Filters

Because OBS Studio lacks an automated color management engine for specific camera sensor profiles, it cannot natively convert proprietary log gamuts into standard target spaces automatically. Instead, conversion is handled manually using the Apply LUT filter.

  • Format and Requirements: OBS uses standard .cube 3D LUT files or PNG-based lookup textures. When working with cinema cameras, a technical conversion LUT (e.g., S-Log3/S-Gamut3.Cine to Rec.709) must be applied directly to the camera's source.
  • Filter Execution Order: The LUT filter evaluates the raw digital numbers from the camera source and maps the flat log curve and wide color gamut directly into the canvas color space (typically Rec. 709 for standard SDR streaming).
  • Processing Precision: OBS applies filters using GPU shaders, executing color mathematics across the texture coordinates before final compositing.

Log-to-HDR Workflows (Rec. 2100)

If streaming or recording in HDR, the OBS canvas can be configured to Rec. 2100 PQ or HLG in the advanced settings:

  • When the canvas is configured for Rec. 2100, log sources can be mapped directly into wide gamuts (such as Rec. 2020) with broad dynamic ranges.
  • In an HDR setup, transform LUTs must be targeted to output Rec. 2100 rather than Rec. 709. Otherwise, the color values will appear washed out or incorrectly mapped against the expanded HDR luminance scale.
  • OBS handles the conversion between color-managed sources and overlays (such as SDR graphics or alerts) via built-in tone mapping shaders, preventing clipping or distortion when combining standard SDR elements with broad-gamut camera footage.
