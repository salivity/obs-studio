Why VFR MP4 Causes Audio Desync in OBS Media Source

Audio desynchronization when playing Variable Frame Rate (VFR) MP4 clips through OBS Studio's default Media Source stems from a fundamental mismatch between fluctuating video timestamps and OBS Studio’s fixed-clock architecture. While consumer media players dynamically stretch or compress playback to keep audio and video aligned, OBS Studio’s internal rendering pipeline is optimized for constant frame rates required by live broadcasts. Consequently, playing VFR footage causes the audio and video tracks to drift apart over time.

Fixed-Clock Processing vs. Variable Timestamps

OBS Studio is built specifically for real-time video compositing and live broadcasting, which relies strictly on Constant Frame Rate (CFR) pipelines (such as 30 or 60 frames per second). In a VFR MP4 file—commonly produced by smartphones, screen recorders, and webcams—the duration of individual frames changes dynamically based on scene complexity or hardware load. When the default Media Source feeds these varying Presentation Timestamps (PTS) into OBS Studio's rigid, fixed-interval rendering loop, the engine cannot cleanly reconcile the shifting video frame durations against the continuous, uninterrupted stream of audio samples.

Decoder and Demuxer Assumptions

The default Media Source in OBS Studio utilizes FFmpeg libraries to demux and decode media files. When decoding an MP4, the player reads container-level metadata to determine the timebase. With VFR files, the decoder often relies on an average frame rate rather than calculating the precise duration of every individual frame in real time. As playback continues, small timing errors—often fractions of a millisecond per frame—accumulate. While the video renderer drops or delays frames to match the canvas refresh rate, the audio decoder outputs uncompressed PCM audio at a fixed sample rate (such as 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz). This causes the video to slowly lag behind or pull ahead of the audio.

Lack of Dynamic Clock Synchronization

Standard media players prevent desync by continuously referencing a master clock—usually the audio clock—and skipping or duplicating video frames dynamically to maintain parity. The default Media Source implementation in OBS Studio lacks aggressive A/V resynchronization routines during local playback. It assumes incoming media conforms to standard broadcast specifications. Furthermore, operations like pausing, looping, or scene switching exacerbate the issue, as the playback engine often fails to calculate the correct timestamp offset upon resuming playback of a VFR stream.

Resolution

Because the OBS Media Source architecture expects linear, predictable timing, the issue cannot be reliably fixed through OBS audio delay filters alone, as the drift increases the longer the clip plays. Resolving the problem requires converting the VFR MP4 file into a Constant Frame Rate (CFR) format using video transcoding software prior to loading it into OBS Studio, or substituting the default Media Source with the VLC Video Source plugin, which handles variable timestamp containers more gracefully.
