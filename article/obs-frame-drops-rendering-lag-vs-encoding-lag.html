OBS Frame Drops: Rendering Lag vs Encoding Lag

OBS Studio distinguishes between different performance bottlenecks by tracking where frames are dropped along its internal pipeline: during scene rendering or during video compression. This article explains how OBS calculates frames lost to rendering lag—which occurs when the GPU cannot composite scenes within the target framerate deadline—compared to frames dropped due to encoding lag, which happens when the CPU or dedicated hardware encoder cannot compress raw frames quickly enough.

The OBS Video Pipeline Overview

To understand how frame drop calculations work, it is necessary to examine the path a single frame takes through OBS Studio:

  1. Scene Composition & Rendering: The GPU gathers sources (capture cards, overlays, browser sources) and renders the final frame to the canvas.
  2. Buffering & Submission: The rendered frame is placed into an internal staging buffer.
  3. Encoding: The encoder (such as x264 on the CPU, or NVENC/AMF/QuickSync on the GPU) compresses the raw frame into an encoded bitstream.
  4. Transmission/Muxing: The encoded packet is sent to the network (streaming) or written to disk (recording).

Bottlenecks at steps 1 and 3 are measured independently using internal clocks and frame counters.

Frames Missed Due to Rendering Lag

Rendering lag occurs when the GPU's 3D/graphics engine is overutilized. OBS requires that each frame be completely drawn within a strict time budget determined by the canvas framerate. For example, at 60 frames per second (FPS), a frame must be rendered in under 16.67 milliseconds.

The Calculation Method

  • Frame Budget Timing: In OBS core (obs-video.c), a high-precision timer tracks the execution time of the rendering loop (render_video).
  • Target Deadline: When the render loop begins, OBS notes the current presentation timestamp (PTS). If scene rendering, scaling, or source filtering takes longer than the allocated frame interval (e.g., >16.66ms for 60 FPS), the GPU misses the presentation deadline.
  • Tallying: If the frame cannot be finished in time to be queued for the current interval, OBS skips drawing that frame entirely to maintain real-time sync with the system clock.
  • The Counter: OBS increments two primary internal variables:
    • total_rendered_frames: Total rendering attempts made.
    • lagged_frames: Frames that failed to render within the timing deadline.
  • The Metric: In the OBS Stats dock, this is presented as: \[\text{Rendering Lag \%} = \left(\frac{\text{lagged\_frames}}{\text{total\_rendered\_frames}}\right) \times 100\]

Because rendering occurs in the general 3D rendering pipeline of the GPU, rendering lag indicates that games or complex canvas filters are exhausting standard GPU processing power.

Skipped Frames Due to Encoding Lag

Encoding lag occurs after a frame has been successfully rendered. Once the GPU outputs a raw frame, that frame is passed to the encoder thread. If the encoder cannot process frames as fast as they arrive, an encoding bottleneck occurs.

The Calculation Method

  • Queue Monitoring: OBS passes completed frames from the rendering thread into an encoder queue managed by obs-encoder.c.
  • Encoder Execution Time: The encoder processes frames sequentially. OBS monitors how long the encoder takes to return a compressed packet. If the encoder takes longer than the framerate budget (e.g., taking 25ms to compress a single frame on a 60 FPS profile), incoming frames begin to back up in the queue.
  • Frame Discarding: To prevent memory bloat and compounding latency, the encoding thread will proactively drop incoming uncompressed frames once the buffer reaches capacity, or skip encoding cycles to allow the hardware to catch up to the current presentation timestamp.
  • The Counter: OBS increments:
    • total_encoded_frames: Total frames fed to the encoder.
    • skipped_frames: Frames dropped prior to or during compression due to the encoder running behind schedule.
  • The Metric: In the OBS Stats dock, this is displayed as: \[\text{Encoding Lag \%} = \left(\frac{\text{skipped\_frames}}{\text{total\_encoded\_frames}}\right) \times 100\]

Because encoding uses either the CPU (via software like x264) or dedicated media silicon (such as NVENC chips on Nvidia cards or AMF on AMD cards), encoding lag indicates that the encoder preset, resolution, or hardware chip capacity is saturated, completely independent of 3D rendering load.

Comparison Summary

Metric Rendering Lag Encoding Lag
Pipeline Stage Pre-encoding (Scene composition) Post-rendering (Compression)
Trigger Mechanism Frame render time exceeds target interval (\(>1/\text{FPS}\)) Encoding thread/hardware cannot keep pace with the input queue
Responsible Component GPU 3D engine and shaders CPU (Software) or Silicon Encoder (ASIC)
OBS Metric Label "Frames missed due to rendering lag" "Skipped frames due to encoding lag"
Resolution Strategy Lower in-game graphics, cap frame rates, or disable heavy OBS filters Lower encoding preset, lower output resolution, or switch to a faster hardware encoder
