Spatial vs Temporal Adaptive Quantization in OBS NVENC

This article explores how OBS Studio implements spatial and temporal adaptive quantization (AQ) within its NVENC encoder wrapper. It details the technical mechanics of both algorithms, explains how OBS communicates these configurations to NVIDIA's Video Codec SDK, and clarifies how these quantization methods alter bit distribution to balance frame complexity, motion, and perceptual visual quality.

The NVENC Wrapper in OBS Studio

OBS Studio interfaces with NVIDIA hardware encoding through a dedicated wrapper (obs-nvenc) that calls the NVIDIA Video Codec SDK (nvEncodeAPI). When configuring an H.264 or HEVC/AV1 stream, OBS populates the NV_ENC_CONFIG and NV_ENC_INITIALIZE_PARAMS structures.

Within these structures, bit distribution is controlled at the macroblock (or Coding Tree Unit) level via Adaptive Quantization. Rather than applying a uniform Quantization Parameter (QP) across an entire frame, AQ dynamically raises or lowers the QP for specific blocks based on perceptual relevance. OBS exposes and controls two distinct variants of this technology: Spatial AQ and Temporal AQ.

Spatial Adaptive Quantization

Spatial AQ analyzes pixel complexity and spatial variance strictly within a single frame. In OBS Studio’s NVENC implementation, enabling spatial adaptive quantization maps to the SDK parameter:

NV_ENC_CONFIG::rcParams.enableAQ = 1;

When this flag is active, the NVENC hardware evaluates the spatial frequency of each block:

  • Flat and Gradient Areas: The human eye easily detects macroblocking and banding in smooth areas, such as skies, shadows, or flat walls. Spatial AQ lowers the QP (assigning more bits) to these blocks to preserve smooth transitions.
  • High-Detail Areas: Complex textures, like foliage or gravel, naturally mask compression artifacts. Spatial AQ increases the QP (assigning fewer bits) to high-detail areas, harvesting bits to spend on sensitive flat regions.

In OBS Studio, this feature is typically exposed directly in the UI as the Spatial AQ or general Adaptive Quantization toggle.

Temporal Adaptive Quantization

Temporal AQ examines pixel relationships and motion vectors across consecutive frames. In the OBS wrapper, this triggers the SDK parameter:

NV_ENC_CONFIG::rcParams.enableTemporalAQ = 1;

Temporal AQ modifies the quantization strategy based on motion predictability:

  • Static and Slow-Moving Content: Objects that remain stationary across frames benefit from sustained high quality, as the eye has time to scrutinize fine details. Temporal AQ lowers the QP on these regions.
  • High-Motion Content: Fast-moving scenes naturally mask low-level detail due to motion blur and the limitations of human visual tracking. Temporal AQ increases the QP on fast-moving macroblocks, diverting bitrate toward the more stable, reference-heavy segments of the stream.

In OBS Studio, Temporal AQ has historically been bundled with or exposed under the Psycho Visual Tuning toggle in the NVENC settings, which also engages rate-distortion optimization (RDO) paths within the encoder.

Practical Bitrate Considerations

In constrained streaming environments (such as low-bitrate CBR targets), enabling both Spatial and Temporal AQ requires careful balance.

Spatial AQ improves the fidelity of static scenes and clean gradients, but it can starve high-motion frames if too many bits are allocated to background details. Conversely, Temporal AQ stabilizes the bitrate during rapid camera pans by sacrificing temporary detail that the eye cannot track. When configured concurrently via OBS Studio, the NVENC hardware blends both passes, optimizing bit allocation dynamically across both space and time before final packetization.
