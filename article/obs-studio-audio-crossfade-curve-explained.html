OBS Studio Audio Crossfade Curve Explained

During scene transitions in OBS Studio, audio channels are blended using a mathematical curve to ensure smooth transitions between sources. Rather than using a simple linear fade, OBS Studio implements an equal-power crossfade based on a sinusoidal curve (quarter-sine and quarter-cosine functions). This article explains the mathematics behind this curve, why it is used instead of a linear slope, and how it prevents volume drops during live broadcasts.

The Mathematical Curve: Equal-Power Sinusoidal

When switching between two scenes with active audio channels in OBS Studio, the transition system applies a trigonometric equal-power curve to the gain of each audio source. Over a normalized time interval \(t\) ranging from \(0.0\) (start of the transition) to \(1.0\) (end of the transition), the audio levels are calculated as follows:

  • Fading-out source gain: \(\cos\left(t \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\)
  • Fading-in source gain: \(\sin\left(t \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\)

At the start (\(t = 0\)), the outgoing audio channel is at full volume (\(\cos(0) = 1\)) and the incoming audio is silent (\(\sin(0) = 0\)). At the midpoint (\(t = 0.5\)), both sources operate at approximately \(0.707\) (or \(-3\text{ dB}\)) of their original amplitude (\(\cos(\pi/4) = \sin(\pi/4) \approx 0.7071\)). At the end (\(t = 1\)), the outgoing audio drops to zero (\(\cos(\pi/2) = 0\)) and the incoming audio reaches unity gain (\(\sin(\pi/2) = 1\)).

Why Equal-Power is Used Instead of Linear Fading

A simple linear crossfade transitions audio amplitude along a straight line:

  • Outgoing source gain: \(1 - t\)
  • Incoming source gain: \(t\)

While linear attenuation works well for video opacity, human hearing perceives sound loudness based on acoustic power rather than raw amplitude. Acoustic power is proportional to the square of the signal amplitude:

\[P \propto A^2\]

If a linear crossfade is applied, at the transition midpoint (\(t = 0.5\)):

\[A_{out}^2 + A_{in}^2 = (0.5)^2 + (0.5)^2 = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5\]

The total acoustic power drops by half, causing a noticeable dip of approximately \(-3\text{ dB}\) in perceived loudness right in the middle of the scene change.

By utilizing the trigonometric identity \(\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1\), the equal-power sinusoidal curve ensures that the total power remains constant across the entire duration:

\[\cos^2\left(t \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right) + \sin^2\left(t \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1\]

Practical Implications for Broadcasters

Because OBS Studio uses an equal-power sinusoidal curve for crossfading audio:

  • Consistent Loudness: Uncorrelated signals (such as different music tracks, ambient sound, or speech from different scenes) maintain a steady, uniform volume throughout the switch.
  • No Center-Dip: Viewers will not perceive an unnatural drop in audio presence when transitioning between scenes.
  • Correlated Audio Precaution: If two scenes share an identical audio source that is not set to remain active across scenes, a brief \(+3\text{ dB}\) boost can occur at the midpoint due to in-phase addition. To prevent this, continuous microphones or desktop audio should be configured as global audio devices or placed inside dedicated nested scenes.
