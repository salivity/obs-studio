Fix OBS Dropped Frames: Wi-Fi Retries vs Bufferbloat

Dropped frames in OBS Studio typically indicate that the encoder output cannot reach the ingest server in time, a problem often caused by either local Wi-Fi packet retries or router bufferbloat. While both issues manifest as network-related dropped frames in OBS, they originate at different points in your network path and require distinct troubleshooting methods. This guide provides a clear diagnostic process using ping tests, network monitoring, and OBS log analysis to pinpoint whether local wireless interference or upstream queue latency is interrupting your stream.

Understanding the Difference

  • Wi-Fi Packet Retries: Occur at the local data link layer (Layer 2) between your PC and the wireless access point. When radio interference, physical barriers, or channel congestion corrupt packets, your Wi-Fi card must repeatedly retransmit them. This causes erratic throughput, micro-stutters, and intermittent local packet loss.
  • Bufferbloat: Occurs at the router or modem level (Layer 3/WAN). When OBS uploads at or near your connection’s bandwidth limit, the router buffers excess packets in an oversized queue instead of dropping them immediately. This introduces massive latency spikes (lag) across the entire network, eventually causing OBS's TCP socket to stall and drop frames.

Step 1: Run Simultaneous Ping Diagnostics

The most reliable way to separate a local wireless issue from router bufferbloat is to isolate your local network segment from the wider internet.

  1. Open two separate Command Prompt or Terminal windows.
  2. In the first window, continuously ping your local router's default gateway: 
    ping -t 192.168.1.1
    (Replace 192.168.1.1 with your actual gateway IP).
  3. In the second window, continuously ping a reliable public DNS server: 
    ping -t 1.1.1.1
  4. Start your stream in OBS Studio at your standard bitrate and monitor both windows.

Interpreting the Results

  • Symptom of Wi-Fi Packet Retries: The ping to your local gateway (192.168.1.1) spikes significantly (e.g., from 2 ms to 150 ms+) or shows "Request timed out," while the external ping mirrors the exact same spikes and drops. Because the local connection to the router is degraded, all outbound traffic suffers.
  • Symptom of Bufferbloat: The ping to your local gateway remains rock-solid (consistently 1 ms to 3 ms with no packet loss), but the ping to the external target (1.1.1.1) climbs dramatically (e.g., from 15 ms to 300 ms+) while streaming. This proves the Wi-Fi link to the router is healthy, but the router's WAN upload queue is overflowing.

Step 2: Check Wi-Fi Adapter Statistics

If the gateway ping indicates a local issue, verify Wi-Fi link degradation directly in your operating system:

  1. Open PowerShell or Command Prompt.
  2. Run the command: 
    netsh wlan show interfaces
  3. Look at the Signal, Receive rate (Mbps), and Transmit rate (Mbps).
    • If the transmit rate fluctuates wildly while OBS is active, or if signal strength drops below 75%, local packet retries are actively throttling your connection.
    • On macOS, hold the Option key and click the Wi-Fi icon on the menu bar to view your Tx Rate and RSSI (values worse than -67 dBm indicate unstable signal quality prone to retries).

Step 3: Test for Bufferbloat Under Load

To confirm bufferbloat independently of OBS:

  1. Stop streaming in OBS.
  2. Connect temporarily via an Ethernet cable (if possible) or ensure you are adjacent to the router to eliminate wireless variables.
  3. Run an online bufferbloat test (such as the Waveform Bufferbloat Test).
  4. If the test returns a grade of C, D, or F, with upload latency increasing by more than 30–50 ms under load, your router's default queue management cannot handle sustained saturation, which will stall OBS uploads.

How to Resolve Each Issue

Resolving Wi-Fi Packet Retries

  • Switch Bands: Move from the congested 2.4 GHz band to a 5 GHz or 6 GHz network.
  • Change Channels: Use a Wi-Fi analyzer app to identify and switch to an uncongested wireless channel.
  • Use Ethernet or MoCA: The definitive fix for packet retries is bypassing wireless transmission entirely using an Ethernet cable or Multimedia over Coax (MoCA) adapter.

Resolving Bufferbloat

  • Enable Dynamic Bitrate in OBS: Go to Settings > Advanced > Network and check Dynamically change bitrate to manage congestion. This prevents the upload queue from filling by reducing stream quality when network stalls occur.
  • Cap OBS Bitrate: Set your maximum streaming bitrate to no more than 70–75% of your verified upload speed.
  • Enable Smart Queue Management (SQM): Configure SQM (such as Cake or FQ-CoDel) in your router settings to actively manage packet queues and keep latency low during maximum upload utilization.
