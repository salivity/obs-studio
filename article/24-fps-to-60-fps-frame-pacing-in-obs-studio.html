24fps to 60fps Frame Pacing in OBS Studio

Playing 24fps cinematic media on a 60fps canvas introduces a mathematical mismatch that can disrupt motion fluidity. This article explains how OBS Studio manages frame pacing across different framerates, detailing how its internal rendering pipeline samples presentation timestamps, uses a 3:2 cadence to prevent temporal drift, and maintains audio-video synchronization without introducing cumulative latency.

The Framerate Mismatch: 24fps on a 60fps Canvas

Cinema standard footage runs at 24 frames per second (or 23.976 fps), while standard broadcast and streaming canvases in OBS Studio typically run at 60 frames per second (or 59.94 fps).

Dividing 60 by 24 results in 2.5 canvas refreshes per video frame. Because a monitor or digital canvas cannot display half a frame, OBS Studio cannot distribute 24 frames symmetrically across 60 discrete render cycles without processing adaptations.

Timestamp-Based Rendering Loop

OBS Studio does not process video by queuing raw frame counts. Instead, it relies on a hardware-clocked rendering loop driven by Presentation Time Stamps (PTS):

  1. The Render Tick: The OBS graphics thread ticks at an exact interval based on the project's base framerate (every 16.67 milliseconds for a 60fps canvas).
  2. PTS Polling: During each tick, the compositor queries every active source—such as a Media Source running via FFmpeg—to retrieve the frame that matches the current master rendering clock.
  3. Decoded Frame Fetching: The decoder continuously unpacks incoming video frames into a memory buffer, tagging each with its native PTS. The rendering engine fetches the newest decoded frame whose timestamp is less than or equal to the current render tick time.

The 3:2 Cadence Implementation

To bridge the 2.5-frame ratio, OBS Studio applies a standard 3:2 pulldown pattern at the compositor level.

  • The first source frame is drawn on the canvas and held for 3 canvas ticks (50ms).
  • The second source frame is drawn and held for 2 canvas ticks (33.33ms).
  • Over 5 canvas frames (83.33ms), exactly 2 video frames are shown (\(3 + 2 = 5\)).
  • Repeating this sequence 12 times produces 60 canvas frames displaying all 24 source frames in one second.

Because OBS evaluates presentation timestamps on every 16.67ms tick, this alternating 3:2 cadence emerges naturally. The compositor never attempts to invent intermediate frames (motion interpolation) or blend frames together unless specialized third-party shaders or filters are applied.

Preventing Audio-Video Drift

Cadence judder (the subtle micro-stutter resulting from alternating between 33.33ms and 50ms display durations) is mathematically unavoidable on a fixed 60Hz grid. However, OBS Studio protects against frame drift—where playback gradually slows down or falls out of sync with audio.

OBS uses audio-driven time synchronization for media playback. The media source’s audio clock acts as the reference timeline. If the video decoding thread falls behind the rendering clock due to high CPU/GPU load, OBS executes a frame drop, skipping the late frame to fetch the current PTS match. Conversely, if frames decode faster than required, they remain buffered until their corresponding timestamp arrives.

Practical Considerations for Smooth Playback

To minimize judder and ensure predictable frame pacing when handling 24fps media inside OBS:

  • Hardware Decoding: Enable hardware decoding in the Media Source properties to prevent decoding bottlenecks that disrupt the 3:2 pattern.
  • Canvas Framerates: If pure, uniform frame pacing is required without a 3:2 cadence, setting the OBS canvas framerate to an integer multiple of 24 (such as 120fps, where each 24fps frame holds for exactly 5 ticks) will display cinema media with perfectly uniform pacing.
