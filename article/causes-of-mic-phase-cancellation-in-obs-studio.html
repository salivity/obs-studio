Causes of Mic Phase Cancellation in OBS Studio

Phase cancellation in OBS Studio occurs when two or more microphones capture the same sound source at slightly different times and are summed together into a single stereo or mono output. This timing discrepancy causes the sound waves to collide, resulting in destructive interference that makes audio sound thin, hollow, or comb-filtered. Understanding the physical and digital causes of this phenomenon is essential for maintaining clear, professional sound quality in multi-microphone live streams and recordings.

Physical Distance and Time Arrival Differences

The primary physical cause of phase cancellation is the acoustic delay between multiple microphones. When a single voice or sound source is captured by two mics placed at unequal distances from the source, the acoustic sound wave reaches the closer microphone milliseconds before it reaches the secondary microphone. When OBS Studio combines these signals into a single stereo track (especially when both are panned toward the center), the peaks of one waveform align with the troughs of the other. Frequencies whose half-wavelengths match this time delay cancel each other out entirely, dramatically reducing the low and midrange frequencies of the voice.

Digital Latency and Hardware Desynchronization

Phase cancellation can also occur digitally within OBS Studio even if microphones are positioned properly in the room. This happens when using audio inputs with different processing latencies, such as:

  • Mixing a USB microphone with an XLR microphone connected through an audio interface.
  • Running inputs through different audio drivers (e.g., mixing ASIO, MME, and WASAPI sources).
  • Applying heavy, unaligned digital signal processing (DSP) or VST plugins to one microphone source but not the other.

Because digital audio is processed in samples, a mismatch of just a few milliseconds creates the exact same phase displacement as physical distance, resulting in comb filtering when mixed into the final track.

Panning and Channel Downmixing

OBS Studio outputs audio based on how channels are routed in the Advanced Audio Properties menu. If multiple microphones are left in their default states, they are typically routed to both the Left and Right channels of the stereo output.

When two separate inputs with overlapping acoustic information are both routed equally to Left and Right (panned dead-center), they are directly summed together. Any phase differences that might have been less noticeable with distinct stereo separation become aggressively apparent, causing immediate frequency loss.

Preventing Phase Cancellation in OBS

To avoid phase cancellation when combining multiple microphone sources:

  • Apply the 3:1 Rule: Keep the distance between two microphones at least three times the distance between each microphone and its respective speaker.
  • Match Hardware and Drivers: Use identical microphones connected through the same multi-channel audio interface rather than combining disparate USB devices.
  • Adjust Sync Offsets: In OBS Studio's Advanced Audio Properties, manually adjust the "Sync Offset (ms)" on individual inputs to align waveforms if slight hardware delays exist.
  • Mute Inactive Mics: Use noise gates or an automixer plugin to attenuate secondary microphones when only one person is speaking.
