OBS Transform Engine: Horizontal Flip Coordinate Math

This article explains how the OBS Studio transform engine processes spatial coordinates when a source is flipped horizontally across its local origin. It breaks down the internal use of 2D affine transformation matrices, scale vector sign inversion, the role of positional alignment anchors, and the step-by-step mathematical pipeline OBS uses to render the flipped source to the canvas.

The Transform Pipeline in OBS Studio

OBS Studio manages the rendering of visual sources inside scenes using standard affine transformation matrices. Every scene item (obs_sceneitem_t) maintains a set of transform properties:

  • Position (\(P\)): The coordinates \((x, y)\) on the canvas.
  • Scale (\(S\)): Multipliers \((s_x, s_y)\) representing width and height scaling factors.
  • Rotation (\(R\)): An angle \(\theta\) in degrees or radians.
  • Alignment Anchor: An integer flag indicating the origin point of the source bounding rectangle (e.g., Top-Left, Center, Bottom-Right).

When a source is rendered, the OBS graphics subsystem calculates a Model-View matrix that maps the raw source pixel coordinates (its local space) into scene canvas coordinates (world space).

Horizontal Flip via Scale Inversion

In OBS Studio, executing a horizontal flip does not alter the underlying texture pixel buffer, nor does it invert the rotation angle. Instead, the engine negates the horizontal scale component:

\[s_x' = -s_x\]

Because reflection is algebraically equivalent to a negative scaling factor along a single Cartesian axis, setting \(s_x\) to a negative value mirrors the geometry along the local vertical axis (\(Y\)-axis) passing through the local origin \((0, 0)\).

Local Origin and Alignment Offsets

Before scale or rotation is applied, OBS evaluates the source's alignment anchor to establish the local origin.

By default, OBS aligns items to the Top-Left (OBS_ALIGN_LEFT | OBS_ALIGN_TOP). In this scenario, the local origin \((0, 0)\) is situated at the top-left corner of the source. If the alignment is centered (OBS_ALIGN_CENTER), the local origin is positioned at \((width / 2, height / 2)\).

Let \((o_x, o_y)\) be the offset from the top-left corner to the chosen anchor point:

  • For Top-Left: \(o_x = 0, \quad o_y = 0\)
  • For Center: \(o_x = \frac{\text{width}}{2}, \quad o_y = \frac{\text{height}}{2}\)

Any point \((u, v)\) in the native source dimensions (\(0 \le u \le \text{width}\), \(0 \le v \le \text{height}\)) is shifted relative to this pivot:

\[p_{\text{local}} = \begin{bmatrix} u - o_x \\ v - o_y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

Mathematical Matrix Construction

The complete transformation matrix \(M\) converts a local texture coordinate \((u, v)\) to the final screen coordinate \((X, Y)\) via the following matrix multiplication sequence:

\[M = T_{\text{position}} \times R_{\text{rotation}} \times S_{\text{scale}} \times T_{\text{origin}}^{-1}\]

Where:

  1. Origin Offset Translation (\(T_{\text{origin}}^{-1}\)): \[T_{\text{origin}}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -o_x \\ 0 & 1 & -o_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

  2. Scaling Matrix (\(S_{\text{scale}}\)) with Flipped \(X\): \[S_{\text{scale}} = \begin{bmatrix} -s_x & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & s_y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

  3. Rotation Matrix (\(R_{\text{rotation}}\)): \[R = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

  4. Position Translation (\(T_{\text{position}}\)): \[T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & P_x \\ 0 & 1 & P_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

Resulting Coordinate Evaluation

When the matrix is evaluated for a point \((u, v)\), the horizontal coordinate before rotation and world translation undergoes the following operation:

\[x_{\text{scaled}} = -s_x \cdot (u - o_x)\]

  • When Centered (\(o_x = \text{width} / 2\)): A point at the left edge (\(u = 0\)) evaluates to \(-s_x \cdot (-\text{width} / 2) = +s_x \cdot (\text{width} / 2)\), projecting it to the right side of the anchor. The point at the right edge (\(u = \text{width}\)) mirrors directly to the left side. The visual center remains locked at \(P_x\).
  • When Top-Left Aligned (\(o_x = 0\)): A point at \(u\) evaluates to \(-s_x \cdot u\). Because all \(u \ge 0\), the coordinates become negative relative to the anchor, extending the rendered frame to the left of the position coordinate \(P_x\) rather than to the right.

To keep items within the anticipated boundaries when utilizing predefined layouts, OBS Studio can automatically adjust the item position \(P_x\) if bounding box locks are active, compensating for the leftward expansion caused by negating the scale against a top-left origin.
