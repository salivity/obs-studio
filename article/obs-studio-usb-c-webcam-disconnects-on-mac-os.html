OBS Studio USB-C Webcam Disconnects on macOS

When a USB-C webcam is disconnected during an active OBS Studio session on macOS, the software relies on the operating system's AVFoundation framework to handle the sudden hardware interruption. This article examines how OBS Studio processes abrupt USB-C disconnections, the visual and technical behaviors that occur on the canvas and in the log files, and how macOS manages device re-enumeration when the camera is plugged back in.

The AVFoundation Interruption Pipeline

OBS Studio interfaces with physical video capture hardware on macOS using Apple's native AVFoundation framework. When a USB-C cable is disconnected mid-stream:

  1. Hardware Interruption: The operating system detects a sudden termination of the USB data lane, generating an I/O disconnect event.
  2. Framework Notification: AVFoundation posts an AVCaptureSessionWasInterruptedNotification to OBS Studio, signaling that the underlying hardware pipeline is no longer providing media frames.
  3. Thread Isolation: OBS processes capture devices on separate capture threads. Because capture logic is decoupled from rendering and output pipelines, the sudden disconnect does not crash the main application, the render loop, or active streaming/recording encoders.

Visual Behavior in the OBS Canvas

When the video stream cuts out, OBS Studio does not replace the source with an error graphic. Instead, one of two visual states occurs depending on driver architecture and software configuration:

  • Last Frame Freeze: By default, OBS retains the last successfully decoded frame in the source buffer. Viewers see a frozen image rather than an immediate blank screen.
  • Black or Transparent Source: If the device driver immediately clears the buffer or if the canvas refreshes the layer without an active buffer cache, the source renders as completely transparent or black.

Downstream outputs—such as active RTMP broadcasts or local recordings—continue running smoothly without dropping the overall stream connection.

Reconnection and Device Re-Enumeration

Reconnecting a USB-C webcam introduces challenges due to how macOS re-allocates device endpoints.

When plugged back in, macOS treats the camera as a newly attached peripheral and assigns it a fresh device identifier. Historically, OBS Studio required users to open the Video Capture Device properties, deactivate the source, and reactivate it to re-hook into AVFoundation.

Recent versions of OBS Studio include enhanced device monitoring. The software listens for AVCaptureDeviceWasConnectedNotification events. If a re-attached webcam matches the unique hardware identifier of the missing source, OBS attempts to rebuild the AVCaptureSession automatically. However, reconnection reliability depends heavily on:

  • Hub Usage: Direct USB-C connections renegotiate faster than connections routed through multi-port USB hubs or Thunderbolt docks.
  • Format Negotiation: If the camera defaults to a different resolution or framerate upon re-initialization, OBS may fail to lock back onto the stream until manually refreshed.

Log File Indicators and Troubleshooting

An unexpected disconnect leaves specific traces in the OBS Studio session log (accessible via Help > Log Files). A typical disconnect event produces errors indicating that the video capture device returned an end-of-stream or missing device status:

[mac-avcapture]: Device disconnected or capture session interrupted

If an unplugged USB-C webcam fails to display video after being reconnected, double-click the camera source in the Sources dock, click Deactivate, wait two seconds for the macOS capture daemon to clear the session, and click Activate to force AVFoundation to re-establish the video feed.
