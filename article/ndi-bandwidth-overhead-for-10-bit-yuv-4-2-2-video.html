NDI Bandwidth Overhead for 10-Bit YUV 4:2:2 Video

This article breaks down the bandwidth requirements and network overhead associated with transmitting 10-bit YUV 4:2:2 video over local NDI networks into OBS Studio. It covers the underlying mathematical formulas for raw data rates, the protocol-level network overhead introduced by transport framing and NDI metadata, and how OBS Studio handles these feeds in local production environments.

Raw Bitrate Calculation for 10-Bit YUV 4:2:2

To determine network overhead, you must first calculate the uncompressed baseband video data rate.

In a YUV 4:2:2 chroma subsampling scheme, the luma channel (Y) is sampled at full resolution, while the two chroma channels (U and V) are horizontally subsampled by half. This results in an average of two samples per pixel. At a bit depth of 10 bits per sample:

  • Bits Per Pixel (bpp): \(2 \text{ samples} \times 10 \text{ bits} = 20 \text{ bpp}\)
  • Bitrate Formula: \(\text{Width} \times \text{Height} \times \text{Frame Rate} \times 20\text{ bits}\)

Baseline Uncompressed Data Rates:

  • 1080p at 60 fps (1920 × 1080): \(1920 \times 1080 \times 60 \times 20 \approx 2.488\text{ Gbps}\)
  • 4K UHD at 60 fps (3840 × 2160): \(3840 \times 2160 \times 60 \times 20 \approx 9.953\text{ Gbps}\)

Network and Transport Protocol Overhead

Streaming video over a local area network wraps raw frames into multiple network layers, each introducing additional data overhead.

  1. Packet Encapsulation (Ethernet, IP, Transport):

    • Standard MTU (1500 bytes): Each network packet carries approximately 1460 bytes of video payload and roughly 40 to 54 bytes of combined Ethernet, IPv4, and TCP/UDP headers. This introduces an overhead of approximately 3% to 4%.
    • Jumbo Frames (9000 bytes): By expanding the payload size per frame, header overhead drops to roughly 0.5% to 1%.

  2. NDI Protocol Packaging and Metadata:

    • NDI transmits frames using either multi-TCP or Reliable UDP (R-UDP).
    • Beyond raw image data, the NDI stream packages clock synchronization, uncompressed floating-point or 24-bit PCM audio, timecodes, and XML-based metadata (such as tally state and source framing info).
    • This structural overhead typically adds another 1% to 2% to the stream.

  3. Total Protocol Overhead: On a standard network configuration (1500 MTU), the combined transport and metadata overhead for streaming an uncompressed bitstream is between 4% and 6%.

Applying this overhead to the baseband calculations:

  • 1080p60 Uncompressed Stream: \(\approx 2.59\text{ Gbps}\) to \(2.64\text{ Gbps}\) of total network throughput.
  • 4K60 Uncompressed Stream: \(\approx 10.35\text{ Gbps}\) to \(10.55\text{ Gbps}\) of total network throughput.

NDI Behavior in OBS Studio

When ingesting feeds via the obs-ndi plugin, it is critical to distinguish between raw uncompressed baseband video and standard "High Bandwidth" NDI:

  • High Bandwidth NDI (SpeedHQ): Standard NDI does not transmit purely uncompressed video. Instead, it utilizes the SpeedHQ intra-frame codec. With the release of NDI 6, 10-bit YUV 4:2:2 HDR pipelines are supported natively. Rather than consuming 2.5 Gbps or 10 Gbps, SpeedHQ compresses the stream at roughly a 15:1 to 20:1 ratio. A 1080p60 10-bit stream typically consumes 150 to 180 Mbps, while 4K60 consumes 300 to 450 Mbps.
  • True Uncompressed Transmission: If bypassing intra-frame compression using custom NDI SDK implementations or raw uncompressed SMPTE ST 2110 bridges into OBS, the overhead requires a minimum of a 10 GbE network link for 1080p60 and a 25 GbE link for 4K60 to prevent packet drops and buffer exhaustion in OBS Studio.
