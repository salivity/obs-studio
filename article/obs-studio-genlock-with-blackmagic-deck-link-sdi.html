OBS Studio Genlock with Blackmagic DeckLink SDI

This article explains how OBS Studio handles genlock synchronization when capturing video through multiple Blackmagic DeckLink SDI interfaces. It details the operational relationship between hardware-level reference clocks, the Blackmagic Desktop Video driver pipeline, and how OBS Studio’s internal frame-compositing engine processes synchronized SDI sources to prevent frame tearing, phase misalignment, and drift in multi-camera productions.

The Role of Hardware-Level Genlock

Genlock (generator locking) is strictly an electrical, hardware-level process rather than a software-driven function. OBS Studio itself cannot generate or synchronize reference clocks. Instead, synchronization is established before frames ever reach the software:

  1. External Reference Signal: A dedicated master sync generator delivers a unified Black Burst (SD/HD) or Tri-Level Sync (HD/UHD) signal via 75-ohm coaxial cables to the REF IN (Reference In) BNC connectors of each Blackmagic DeckLink card or card channel.
  2. Phase Alignment: The DeckLink hardware phase-locks its internal analog-to-digital converters and SDI decoders to the reference signal.
  3. Coordinated Capture: Because the input cameras and the DeckLink interfaces share identical line and frame timing, every SDI frame across all connected inputs arrives at the hardware level simultaneously, with identical vertical interval timing.

Driver and SDK Frame Ingestion

Once the DeckLink hardware captures phase-locked frames, the Blackmagic Desktop Video driver and SDK process the incoming streams.

  • Hardware Timestamps: As each frame is ingested, the DeckLink driver tags it with a high-precision hardware presentation timestamp based on the locked clock.
  • Zero Clock Drift: When cards are not genlocked, slight oscillator variations cause inputs to drift apart over time, leading to occasional dropped or duplicated frames. With genlock enabled, the DeckLink driver delivers audio and video samples to the operating system at mathematically identical rates across all channels.

How OBS Studio Manages Synchronized DeckLink Inputs

OBS Studio integrates with Blackmagic hardware natively via its DeckLink source plugin, built using the Blackmagic DeckLink SDK.

Presentation and Buffering

OBS Studio does not slave its primary rendering clock to an external SDI sync; its internal graphics pipeline runs decoupled from external video clocks, driven by the system GPU and internal timer. However, OBS handles the multi-input DeckLink streams effectively through the following mechanisms:

  • Direct SDK Polling: Rather than routing streams through generic DirectShow or Media Foundation layers (which introduce unpredictable latency and jitter), OBS pulls uncompressed frame buffers directly from the Blackmagic API.
  • Timestamp Alignment: OBS reads the hardware-level timestamps provided by the DeckLink SDK. Because the inputs are hardware-synchronized, their arrival intervals align consistently with OBS Studio’s rendering loop.
  • Buffering Modes: In the OBS DeckLink source properties, disabling the "Buffering" option allows OBS to render the most recent frame immediately upon arrival. Since the inputs share identical phase timing, switching between sources or displaying them simultaneously (e.g., side-by-side or picture-in-picture) results in synchronized playback without visible phase offset.

Recommended Configuration for Frame-Accurate Performance

To ensure OBS Studio maintains alignment across multiple genlocked DeckLink inputs:

  • Match Project Framerates: Set OBS Studio's canvas framerate (in Settings > Video) to match the exact fractional or integer framerate of the SDI broadcast standard in use (e.g., 59.94 fps, 50 fps, or 29.97 fps).
  • Verify Lock in Desktop Video Setup: Ensure that the Blackmagic Desktop Video Setup utility reports a verified "Reference: Locked" status for every assigned SDI channel.
  • Standardize Format Settings: Configure all cameras, capture channels, and internal OBS sources to identical video modes (resolution, colorspace, and framerate) to eliminate per-input scaler and frame-rate conversion latencies.
