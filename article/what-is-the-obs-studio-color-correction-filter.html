What is the OBS Studio Color Correction Filter?

The color correction filter in OBS Studio is a built-in tool used to adjust and enhance the visual appearance of video sources, such as webcams, game capture, and cameras. By altering settings like brightness, contrast, and saturation, creators can fix poor lighting, match colors between different cameras, and apply creative color grading to their live streams or recordings. This article explains the primary functions of this filter, its key settings, and how to apply it to your sources.

Core Functions of the Color Correction Filter

The primary purpose of the color correction filter is to improve video quality directly within OBS Studio without needing external hardware or post-production editing software. It is commonly used for:

  • Correcting Poor Lighting: Compensating for rooms that are too dark, too bright, or lit by inconsistent light sources.
  • Camera Matching: Ensuring that multiple cameras or webcams have consistent color temperatures and exposure levels.
  • Creative Branding: Giving a stream a specific mood, such as a warm, cozy look or a cool, futuristic aesthetic.
  • Color Keying Preparation: Adjusting the contrast and saturation of a green screen source to make chroma keying more accurate.

Key Settings Explained

When you apply the color correction filter to a source, you are presented with several sliders. Each controls a specific aspect of the image:

  • Gamma: Adjusts the mid-tones of the video. Increasing gamma brightens the darker areas of the image without blowing out the highlights.
  • Contrast: Adjusts the difference between the darkest and lightest parts of the image. Higher contrast makes shadows darker and highlights brighter, adding depth to a flat image.
  • Brightness: Adjusts the overall light level of the entire video source.
  • Saturation: Controls the intensity of the colors. Lowering saturation to zero creates a black-and-white effect, while increasing it makes colors more vibrant.
  • Hue Shift: Rotates the color spectrum of the video. This is useful for correcting off-color tints (like a camera displaying skin tones as too green or blue).
  • Opacity: Controls the transparency of the video source, allowing underlying sources to show through.
  • Color Multiply: Tints the source with a specific selected color, which is useful for dramatic atmospheric lighting effects.

How to Apply the Filter in OBS Studio

Adding the color correction filter to your source takes only a few steps:

  1. Right-click the video source (e.g., your webcam) in the Sources dock.
  2. Select Filters from the context menu.
  3. Click the + (plus) icon in the bottom-left corner of the "Effect Filters" section.
  4. Select Color Correction from the list, name it, and click OK.
  5. Adjust the sliders in the properties window while watching the real-time preview until you achieve the desired visual result.
