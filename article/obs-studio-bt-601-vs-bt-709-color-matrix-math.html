OBS Studio: BT.601 vs BT.709 Color Matrix Math

This article examines the mathematical differences between the BT.601 and BT.709 color matrix implementations inside OBS Studio. It covers the specific luma and chroma transformation formulas, how OBS Studio handles these calculations in its graphics subsystem shaders, the interaction with dynamic range settings, and the visual impact of mathematical mismatches during video compositing.

Fundamental Luma and Chroma Coefficients

Inside OBS Studio, color conversions between RGB and YUV color spaces rely on color transformation matrices defined by international standards. The fundamental difference between ITU-R BT.601 (standard definition) and ITU-R BT.709 (high definition) lies in the weighting coefficients assigned to the Red (\(R\)), Green (\(G\)), and Blue (\(B\)) spectral components to calculate the luma (\(Y\)) signal.

The standard luma equations are:

  • BT.601:
    \[Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B\]
  • BT.709:
    \[Y = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B\]

Because the chroma channels (\(U\)/\(Cb\) and \(V\)/\(Cr\)) represent color difference signals derived as \(B - Y\) and \(R - Y\), changes to the luma weighting alter the entire transformation matrix.

For normalized color spaces, the chroma difference values are derived as follows:

  • BT.601:
    \[U = \frac{B - Y}{2(1 - 0.114)} = \frac{B - Y}{1.772} \approx -0.1687R - 0.3313G + 0.5000B\]
    \[V = \frac{R - Y}{2(1 - 0.299)} = \frac{R - Y}{1.402} \approx 0.5000R - 0.4187G - 0.0813B\]
  • BT.709:
    \[U = \frac{B - Y}{2(1 - 0.0722)} = \frac{B - Y}{1.8556} \approx -0.1146R - 0.3854G + 0.5000B\]
    \[V = \frac{R - Y}{2(1 - 0.2126)} = \frac{R - Y}{1.5748} \approx 0.5000R - 0.4542G - 0.0458B\]

BT.709 gives significantly greater weight to the green channel (\(0.7152\) versus \(0.5870\)) and reduced weight to both red (\(0.2126\) versus \(0.2990\)) and blue (\(0.0722\) versus \(0.1140\)), reflecting the chromaticities of modern display phosphors and sRGB/Rec.709 primaries.

YUV to RGB Conversion Matrices

When OBS Studio renders incoming video frames (such as from capture cards, media sources, or webcams) to the RGB canvas, it executes an inverse matrix operation on the GPU.

The unscaled inverse transformation formulas for normalized ranges are:

BT.601 Inverse Math

  • \(R = Y + 1.402V\)
  • \(G = Y - 0.344136U - 0.714136V\)
  • \(B = Y + 1.772U\)

BT.709 Inverse Math

  • \(R = Y + 1.5748V\)
  • \(G = Y - 0.187324U - 0.468124V\)
  • \(B = Y + 1.8556U\)

OBS Studio Pipeline Implementation

OBS Studio handles these transformations using graphics shaders in the libobs core (primarily implemented across HLSL and GLSL shader files such as color.effect and matrix utility functions in matrix.c).

Instead of computing conversions on the CPU, OBS constructs a 4x4 or 4x3 transformation matrix passed to GPU shader constants. The mathematical operations combine three distinct stages into a single matrix multiplication per pixel:

  1. Offsetting: Subtraction of the chroma bias (shifting unsigned \(U\) and \(V\) values by \(-0.5\) or \(-128\)).
  2. Range Expansion: Scaling factors based on whether the format uses "Partial" (Limited: 16–235 for Y, 16–240 for UV) or "Full" (0–255 for all channels).
  3. Color Space Conversion: Direct evaluation of the BT.601 or BT.709 matrix coefficients.

When "Limited" range is selected in OBS, the matrix mathematically accounts for quantization scaling before applying matrix weights:

  • Luma scale: \(\frac{255}{235 - 16} = \frac{255}{219} \approx 1.16438\)
  • Chroma scale: \(\frac{255}{240 - 16} = \frac{255}{224} \approx 1.13839\)

When combined into the final shader execution matrix, the BT.601 Limited and BT.709 Limited transformations exhibit distinct matrix row entries:

  • BT.601 Limited to RGB (Matrix Row for Red):
    \(R = 1.16438 \times (Y - 16) + 1.59603 \times (V - 128)\)
  • BT.709 Limited to RGB (Matrix Row for Red):
    \(R = 1.16438 \times (Y - 16) + 1.79274 \times (V - 128)\)

The green matrix row exhibits an even larger deviation:

  • BT.601 Limited to RGB (Green):
    \(G = 1.16438 \times (Y - 16) - 0.39176 \times (U - 128) - 0.81297 \times (V - 128)\)
  • BT.709 Limited to RGB (Green):
    \(G = 1.16438 \times (Y - 16) - 0.21325 \times (U - 128) - 0.53291 \times (V - 128)\)

Mathematical Mismatch Impact

Selecting the incorrect matrix in OBS Studio settings produces mathematical cross-talk errors in the color reproduction:

  • BT.709 content interpreted as BT.601: The mathematical weight assigned to red chroma (\(1.402\) vs \(1.5748\)) is too low, causing reds to appear desaturated and slightly shifted toward orange. Green appears overly saturated and slightly darker due to the excessive subtraction from the higher BT.601 coefficients (\(0.344\) and \(0.714\) vs \(0.187\) and \(0.468\)).
  • BT.601 content interpreted as BT.709: Red saturation increases excessively past broadcast-safe boundaries, and skin tones shift unnaturally toward magenta because the \(V\) component is amplified by \(1.5748\) instead of \(1.402\).
