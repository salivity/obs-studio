OBS NVENC LLHQ Preset: Latency and Quality Impact

The Low Latency High Quality (LLHQ) preset in OBS Studio’s NVENC encoder optimizes video output for real-time streaming by minimizing the delay between frame capture and encoding while preserving as much visual fidelity as possible. By reducing internal frame buffering and disabling or strictly limiting bidirectional predictive frames (B-frames), LLHQ delivers an instantaneous stream suited for interactive broadcasting, though it trades off a minor amount of compression efficiency compared to standard high-quality presets.

How the LLHQ Preset Functions

When using NVIDIA's hardware encoder (NVENC), traditional high-quality presets buffer multiple frames in advance to analyze motion vectors and calculate B-frames. This lookahead buffer improves compression efficiency, providing a sharper image at a given bitrate.

The LLHQ preset alters this pipeline by instructing the NVENC chip to process frames with minimal queueing. It restricts deep frame reordering and minimizes encoding latency to single-digit milliseconds per frame.

Impact on Latency

The primary benefit of selecting LLHQ is a noticeable reduction in stream delay. Standard encoding pipelines introduce display latency due to frame buffering. Under LLHQ:

  • Buffer Depth is Minimized: The encoder does not hold frames in memory waiting for future frames to calculate bidirectional references.
  • Pipeline Delay Drops: Video packets are generated and pushed to the RTMP/SRT/WebRTC ingest server almost instantaneously.
  • Interaction Improves: Streamers who rely on near-instant chat interaction, remote desktop streaming, or cloud gaming setups experience significantly less perceived lag.

Impact on Image Quality

While LLHQ attempts to preserve quality ("High Quality"), the structural limitations of low-latency encoding introduce visual trade-offs:

  • Lower Compression Efficiency: Because B-frames are either reduced or removed, the encoder must rely more heavily on I-frames and P-frames. P-frames require more data to achieve the same visual clarity as B-frames.
  • Artifacting in High-Motion Scenes: At lower bitrates (such as standard Twitch limits of 6,000 kbps), high-motion scenes—like fast-paced first-person shooters—may exhibit slightly more pixelation, macroblocking, or softness compared to the standard High Quality (HQ) preset.
  • Compensating with Bitrate: To match the visual quality of standard presets, an LLHQ stream generally requires a 10% to 15% higher bitrate if the platform allows it.

Impact on System Performance

From a hardware standpoint, LLHQ is light on system resources. Because the encoder does not maintain large lookahead queues in VRAM, memory overhead is marginally lower. Modern NVIDIA GPUs (Turing, Ampere, Ada Lovelace) handle LLHQ effortlessly via dedicated NVENC silicon, meaning in-game framerates (FPS) remain virtually unaffected, provided the GPU is not otherwise saturated at 100% load.

When to Use LLHQ

  • Real-Time Streaming Protocols: Environments utilizing WebRTC, FTL, or Low-Latency HLS where end-to-end delay must remain under two seconds.
  • Interactive Content: Live auctions, Q&A sessions, or fast-paced community gaming where immediate audience response is critical.
  • Remote Production: Sending an NDI or SRT feed to a secondary production PC or studio monitor for live monitoring.

If ultra-low latency is not a strict requirement—such as when recording offline video or broadcasting to platforms with standard 5-to-10-second delays—standard Quality or Max Quality presets remain the superior choice for maximum visual fidelity.
