How OBS Studio Uses RNNoise for Noise Suppression

OBS Studio provides real-time audio filtering by embedding the open-source RNNoise library, an artificial intelligence model designed to eliminate ambient background noise from microphone feeds. By pairing a lightweight recurrent neural network with classical digital signal processing, OBS detects human speech patterns and attenuates non-vocal audio—such as mechanical keyboards, computer fans, and ambient room hum—allowing creators to broadcast clean voice audio with low latency and modest computational overhead.

The RNNoise Architecture

RNNoise was developed by Jean-Marc Valin through the Xiph.Org Foundation. Unlike heavy, end-to-end deep learning models that process raw audio waveforms directly, RNNoise relies on a hybrid framework. It combines standard digital signal processing (DSP) techniques with a compact neural network made up of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and dense layers.

Traditional DSP manages the mathematical transformations, while the neural network handles the complex task of identifying which frequency components contain human speech versus unwanted noise. This hybrid approach enables the model to occupy less than 1 megabyte of memory, making it practical for real-time applications like OBS Studio.

Audio Feature Extraction

When an audio stream enters the OBS noise suppression filter using the RNNoise method, it is broken down into 10-millisecond frames. The process follows several specific steps:

  1. Band Division: The algorithm uses a filter bank based on the Bark scale, which models the human ear's non-linear frequency perception. The spectrum is divided into 22 critical frequency bands.
  2. Feature Computation: The filter computes pitch correlation, spectral shape, and energy variations for each band.
  3. Complexity Reduction: By transforming thousands of raw samples into just 22 spectral bands alongside pitch features, the data volume fed into the neural network is dramatically reduced, eliminating the need for a dedicated GPU.

Gated Recurrent Units and Spectral Masking

Once features are extracted, they are passed through the neural network:

  • Speech Probability: The GRU layers evaluate the spectral features across consecutive time steps. Because GRUs retain a memory of previous audio frames, they can track continuous phonemes and distinguish persistent speech from irregular noise bursts.
  • Gain Prediction: Instead of generating new audio, the network outputs 22 gain values (ranging from 0.0 to 1.0), representing a continuous spectral mask for each Bark band. If a band primarily contains vocal frequencies, its gain remains near 1.0; if it consists solely of steady hum or clicking, its gain drops toward 0.0.

Signal Reconstruction and Output

After predicting the gains, the DSP layer applies them to the original frequency domain representation of the input audio. An Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT) converts the filtered spectrum back into a standard time-domain waveform using an overlap-add method. This reconstructed audio stream, free of non-speech components, is then passed to the OBS audio mixer for streaming or recording.

Why RNNoise Excels in OBS Studio

Traditional noise suppression methods, such as standard noise gates or spectral subtraction, rely heavily on static thresholds or stationary noise profiles. They often struggle with non-stationary sounds—like typing, chair creaks, or traffic—and can leave behind noticeable robotic or "underwater" artifacts.

RNNoise excels in OBS Studio because its neural network was trained on vast datasets of varied speech and noise types. It adapts dynamically to changing background conditions on the fly, maintaining natural voice characteristics without requiring the user to manually configure thresholds or noise profiles.
