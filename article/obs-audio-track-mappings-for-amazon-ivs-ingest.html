OBS Audio Track Mappings for Amazon IVS Ingest

This article explains how OBS Studio handles audio track mapping and data transmission when broadcasting multi-channel or multi-track feeds to an Amazon Interactive Video Service (Amazon IVS) ingest point. Because standard ingest relies on the RTMP/RTMPS protocol, there is no bidirectional track negotiation between the encoder and the ingest server. Instead, OBS Studio unilaterally maps, encodes, and packages audio streams into container metadata, while Amazon IVS parses these definitions according to its ingest specifications.

The Mechanism: Declaration Rather Than Negotiation

In protocols like WebRTC, audio capabilities and track allocations are negotiated bi-directionally via Session Description Protocol (SDP) offers and answers. Amazon IVS RTMP/RTMPS ingest operates differently:

  • Unidirectional Signaling: OBS Studio does not negotiate with the Amazon IVS ingest server. Instead, OBS dictates the audio layout and announces it via the initial onMetaData RTMP packet during stream initialization.
  • Ingest Evaluation: Once connected, the Amazon IVS ingest endpoint inspects the elementary audio stream descriptors (AAC headers) and the container metadata. If the stream parameters fall within supported bounds (AAC-LC, 48 kHz sample rate, up to 160 kbps for Basic channels or 320 kbps for Standard channels), IVS ingests the track as declared.

OBS Studio Internal Mapping: Multi-Track vs. Multi-Channel

To understand how audio arrives at IVS, a distinction must be made between multi-track audio and multi-channel audio within OBS Studio:

  1. Multi-Track Audio (Discrete Audio Feeds):

    • Configured via Edit > Advanced Audio Properties.
    • OBS allows sources to be routed to six discrete tracks.
    • Standard RTMP historically supports only one audio track. In Settings > Output > Streaming, OBS assigns a single designated track (typically Track 1) to the RTMP muxer.
    • Secondary tracks (Tracks 2–6) are ignored by standard RTMP streaming outputs and will not reach Amazon IVS unless an alternative transport protocol supporting multi-track (such as Enhanced RTMP) is used and supported end-to-end.

  2. Multi-Channel Audio (Surround Sound / Channel Layout):

    • Configured via Settings > Audio > General > Channels (e.g., Stereo, 2.1, 4.0, 5.1, or 7.1).
    • When multi-channel audio is enabled, OBS bundles all assigned audio channels into a single elementary AAC bitstream on the active streaming track.
    • The channel configuration is embedded directly into the MPEG-4 AudioSpecificConfig header, defining the layout (such as front-left, front-right, center, LFE, surround).

How Amazon IVS Interprets Multi-Channel Feeds

Amazon IVS is engineered primarily for widespread consumer playback across web and mobile browsers:

  • Stereo Normalization: Amazon IVS strictly supports 2-channel stereo audio for standard playback pipelines.
  • Ingest Behavior for Surround Formats: If OBS Studio sends a 5.1 or 7.1 multi-channel feed, Amazon IVS does not map these into discrete user-selectable tracks. Depending on the channel processing tier, the ingest point will either downmix the surround channels into a standard 2-channel stereo profile or pass the surround format directly to transcoded renditions, which frequently causes playback failure or channel dropping on client devices lacking multi-channel hardware decoders.
  • Secondary Track Omission: Because standard Amazon IVS RTMP ingest targets a single elementary audio stream, any secondary audio streams multiplexed into the container without prior configuration are discarded by the ingest parser.

Recommended OBS Configuration for Amazon IVS

To ensure predictable ingest and playback behavior without lost channels:

  • Audio Channels: Set Settings > Audio > Channels to Stereo.
  • Audio Track Routing: In Advanced Audio Properties, mix all relevant audio sources intended for the audience down into Track 1.
  • Streaming Track Selection: In Settings > Output, set the Streaming Audio Track to 1.
  • Codec and Sample Rate: Configure audio encoding to AAC with a sample rate of 48 kHz to match IVS transcode defaults natively.
