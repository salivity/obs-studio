OBS Studio Peak vs True RMS Metering Explained

OBS Studio relies on audio meters to help creators manage levels, avoid digital distortion, and deliver balanced sound. The primary difference between peak metering and True RMS (Root Mean Square) metering lies in what each algorithm calculates: peak metering measures the absolute highest instantaneous voltage level of an audio signal, whereas RMS metering calculates the average continuous energy of the signal over time. Understanding how OBS Studio implements both algorithms ensures your broadcast remains clear, audible, and free of clipping.

Understanding Peak Metering

Peak metering focuses strictly on transients—the short, sudden bursts of sound such as drum hits, plosives (P and B sounds), or sudden shouts. In digital audio, signals cannot exceed 0 dBFS (decibels relative to full scale) without experiencing harsh digital clipping.

In OBS Studio, peak metering tracks the highest discrete sample values in the audio stream:

  • Algorithmic Behavior: The algorithm continuously monitors individual audio samples and registers the highest absolute amplitude value at any given instant. OBS also incorporates a "peak hold" mechanism—represented by the small moving tick mark at the leading edge of the meter—to show the highest level reached over a recent decay period.
  • Primary Purpose: Preventing clipping. Because peak meters react instantaneously, they inform you whether a sudden peak will exceed the digital ceiling and distort your stream or recording.

Understanding True RMS Metering

True RMS metering is based on a mathematical formula that calculates the continuous power of an audio waveform. Human hearing does not perceive loudness based on instant microsecond spikes; instead, our ears respond to integrated acoustic energy sustained over time.

  • Algorithmic Behavior: The RMS algorithm takes the audio samples across a defined time window, squares their values, calculates the arithmetic mean of those squares, and then takes the square root of that average.
  • Primary Purpose: Gauging perceived loudness. An audio source with high transient peaks but low RMS energy (like a click track) will look loud on a peak meter but sound quiet to listeners. Conversely, an audio source with consistent energy (like a compressed vocal or steady background music) will register higher on an RMS scale, closely matching what the audience actually hears.

How OBS Studio Implements Both in the Audio Mixer

Rather than forcing users to pick only one measurement, the OBS Studio audio mixer integrates both measurements visually into the volume fader:

  1. The Solid Bar (RMS): The colored solid bar (transitioning through green, yellow, and red) illustrates the average energy/RMS level of the signal. This helps you balance ongoing levels so dialogue consistently rests comfortably above background noise and music.
  2. The Moving Tick Mark (Peak): The trailing tick mark displays peak signal levels. If this tick reaches the far right edge (0 dBFS), your signal is clipping, even if the solid RMS bar is lingering comfortably in the green or yellow zones.

Practical Application for Streamers

  • Use Peak to Set Headroom: Adjust the gain on your microphone and desktop audio so peak ticks consistently avoid the red zone. Leaving 3 dB to 6 dB of headroom prevents unexpected laughter or game explosions from distorting.
  • Use RMS to Balance Mixes: Ensure your voice's solid RMS level sits in the upper yellow range (-15 dBFS to -10 dBFS), while background music or game audio sits in the green range (-25 dBFS to -20 dBFS). This ensures vocal intelligibility regardless of transient spikes.
