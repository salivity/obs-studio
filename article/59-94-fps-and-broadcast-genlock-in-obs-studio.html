59.94 FPS and Broadcast Genlock in OBS Studio

Setting the canvas frame rate to a fractional value like 59.94 frames per second (fps) in OBS Studio bridges the timing gap between software-based rendering and traditional NTSC broadcast infrastructure. While OBS Studio cannot natively slave its internal render loop to a physical hardware genlock reference signal, configuring the canvas to precise fractional timing minimizes frame drops, frame duplications, and audio drift when ingesting or outputting video through professional SDI and SMPTE-compliant hardware.

Fractional Frame Rates vs. Whole Numbers

In North American and Japanese broadcast standards (NTSC), standard high-definition video runs at a fractional rate derived from the formula 60 × (1000 / 1001), which is approximately 59.94 fps. In contrast, standard computer displays and consumer digital video run at an exact integer rate of 60.00 fps.

If OBS Studio is set to an integer 60.00 fps while interfacing with a 59.94 fps broadcast feed, there is a discrepancy of roughly 3.6 frames every minute. Over time, this discrepancy forces the software or output capture card to drop or duplicate frames to maintain synchronization, which causes visible stuttering and eventual audio desynchronization.

The Limits of Genlock in Software

True broadcast genlock (generator locking) synchronizes video equipment down to the exact horizontal and vertical blanking intervals using an external reference signal, such as Black Burst or Tri-Level Sync. This ensures that multiple video sources switch seamlessly without frame buffers or visual tearing.

OBS Studio operates within standard desktop operating systems (Windows, macOS, Linux) that rely on internal system clocks rather than hardware sync pulses. As a result:

  • OBS cannot achieve phase-accurate genlock: OBS cannot force its graphics pipeline to begin rendering a frame at the exact microsecond an external reference pulse arrives.
  • Clock drift still occurs: Even when configured to 59.94 fps, the computer’s motherboard or GPU clock will drift slightly relative to an external master broadcast clock.

How 59.94 FPS Improves Hardware Compatibility

Despite the lack of true phase-level genlock, selecting fractional frame rates directly impacts how OBS interacts with professional broadcast capture and playback cards (such as Blackmagic DeckLink or AJA devices):

  1. Cadence Matching: Configuring the OBS canvas to fractional 59.94 fps aligns the frame delivery cadence with the expected refresh rate of broadcast hardware. This allows SDI output cards to read frames from the buffer at the same rate OBS produces them.
  2. Reduced Buffer Overflows and Underflows: When the frame rates match, the hardware frame store on an SDI output card remains stable. The card is far less likely to run out of frames (causing a glitch/black frame) or accumulate excess frames (increasing transmission latency).
  3. Cleaner Frame Rate Conversion (FRC): If an external frame synchronizer or broadcast switcher is placed downstream from the OBS system, feeding it a clean 59.94 fps signal allows the hardware synchronizer to lock the signal to the house reference with only an occasional, imperceptible single-frame drop or repeat, rather than continuous stutter.

Recommended OBS Settings for Broadcast Workflows

To optimize OBS Studio for broadcast environments requiring fractional timing:

  • Navigate to Settings > Video.
  • Set FPS Values to Fractional FPS Value rather than "Common FPS Values."
  • Set the Numerator to 60000 and the Denominator to 1001 (yielding 59.94 fps) instead of relying on rounded decimal inputs.
  • Ensure all physical capture cards and camera inputs are explicitly configured to 59.94 fps or 1080i 59.94 to avoid internal resampling inside OBS.
