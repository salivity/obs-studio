OBS DeckLink Output: Routing Clean Video Feeds

The DeckLink Output integration in OBS Studio enables broadcasters to transmit uncompressed, low-latency video directly to dedicated Blackmagic Design hardware, facilitating the separation of raw video from graphic overlays. This article explores how the DeckLink Output plugin functions to route a "clean feed" (program video without graphics, alerts, or lower thirds), the architectural setup required within OBS scenes, and the operational advantages this routing provides for live multi-destination production and post-production workflows.

Understanding Clean vs. Dirty Program Feeds

In live broadcast environments, a standard stream feed typically includes graphics, sponsor logos, chat boxes, lower thirds, and interactive overlays; this is known as a "dirty feed." Conversely, a "clean feed" consists exclusively of the primary visual content—such as camera angles, gameplay, or remote video callers—completely stripped of visual overlays.

Routing a clean feed is essential when distributing content to external switchers, secondary broadcast destinations, in-venue screens (IMAG), or archival systems where permanent graphics would hinder re-editing or localized branding.

The Role of the DeckLink Output Plugin

OBS Studio integrates with Blackmagic Design DeckLink capture and playback cards via native output functionality and specialized source filters. The DeckLink Output plugin fulfills several technical roles:

  • Hardware Baseband Routing: It bypasses standard software encoding (like H.264/HEVC) and converts the internal digital frame buffer directly into standard broadcast baseband signals (SDI or HDMI).
  • Dedicated Channel Assignment: On multi-channel cards (such as the DeckLink Duo 2 or Quad 2), the plugin allows operators to assign distinct OBS video targets to specific physical BNC/SDI connectors.
  • Source and Scene Level Tapping: Rather than being restricted to the master Program view, advanced DeckLink implementations allow OBS to tap into specific scenes or nested sources before graphics are composited onto the final mix.

How to Route Clean Video Without Overlays

Achieving a clean output alongside a dirty master stream relies on scene nesting combined with the DeckLink plugin's routing capability:

  1. Construct the Clean Scene: Create a base scene containing only the raw production elements (cameras, media sources, captures). This scene acts as the "Clean Program."
  2. Apply DeckLink Hardware Output:
    • By using a dedicated DeckLink Render Filter on the clean scene, OBS routes the visual data of that specific container directly to an SDI/HDMI port on the DeckLink card.
    • Alternatively, if using the global DeckLink Output tool (Tools > DeckLink Output), set the main OBS Program to monitor the clean visuals while delegating dirty graphics to a separate preview or secondary window, depending on the plugin version.
  3. Nest the Clean Scene into a Dirty Scene: Create a secondary "Dirty Program" scene, insert the Clean Scene as a source, and layer all graphics, widgets, and overlays on top.
  4. Distribute Independently: The dirty scene is selected as the primary output for the RTMP/SRT stream to web platforms, while the DeckLink hardware simultaneously pushes the pristine clean feed to hardware switchers, recorders, or auxiliary monitors.

Operational Benefits

  • Post-Production Flexibility: Archiving a clean feed directly over SDI to an external recorder (such as a Blackmagic HyperDeck) ensures editing teams have access to footage without baked-in overlays, allowing re-use for highlight reels, promotional assets, or alternative aspect-ratio cuts.
  • Multi-Language and Syndication Distribution: Broadcasters can send an unbranded video signal to co-streamers or regional partners who need to insert their own localized graphics, commentary, and advertising.
  • Low-Latency In-House Display: Sending video to arena screens or confidence monitors via DeckLink avoids the encoding and decoding latency introduced by networked or software-compressed streams, ensuring near-instantaneous visual delivery.
