Why Thin Lines Flicker with Bilinear Filtering in OBS

Flickering and shimmering artifacts on thin line graphics in OBS Studio are primarily caused by temporal aliasing, which occurs when high-frequency visual details are downscaled or sampled using bilinear filtering. When thin elements—such as wireframes, user interface borders, code text, or fine geometric grids—are scaled down, their width often drops below the sampling threshold of the display grid. Because bilinear filtering uses an extremely small sampling area, slight frame-to-frame shifts in position or rounding cause these thin lines to alternately register and disappear, resulting in visible flickering.

Understanding the Bilinear Filtering Mechanism

Bilinear filtering is a lightweight interpolation algorithm designed to smooth textures by taking a weighted average of a 2x2 grid of adjacent pixels. While computationally efficient, its small 4-pixel sampling kernel is its primary weakness when dealing with high-frequency details.

When a window capture is scaled down, multiple source pixels must be compressed into a single destination pixel. A line that is only one or two pixels wide in the source capture may occupy only a fraction of a destination pixel. Because bilinear interpolation does not evaluate the entire area covered by the downscaled pixel, it frequently misses or under-weights the line depending on where the sampling points land.

The Role of Sub-Pixel Shifts and Temporal Aliasing

Flickering is a form of temporal aliasing—aliasing that changes over time. Window captures are prone to minute, sub-pixel coordinate shifts caused by:

  • Dynamic rendering updates from the captured application.
  • Non-integer scaling factors (e.g., scaling a 1440p window to fit an arbitrary layout space on a 1080p canvas).
  • Frame rate mismatches between the source window and the OBS canvas.

As an element moves across sub-pixel boundaries—even by fractions of a pixel—the 2x2 sample grid alternately captures the thin line with high intensity in one frame, and then almost entirely misses it in the next. This rapid oscillation between dark and bright pixel states produces a visible strobing or shimmering effect.

Inadequate Anti-Aliasing for Downscaling

Downscaling high-resolution details requires adequate low-pass filtering to remove frequencies higher than half the sampling rate (the Nyquist limit). Bilinear filtering does not function as an effective low-pass filter when downscaling significantly. Instead of properly integrating all the source pixels within a target pixel's footprint, it skips large amounts of image data, creating severe moiré patterns and structural flickering along thin geometry.

How to Prevent Flickering in OBS Studio

To eliminate flickering artifacts on thin lines in OBS Studio, you must either change how the source is sampled or remove the scaling discrepancy:

  • Switch to the Area Scale Filter: Right-click the captured source in OBS, navigate to Scale Filtering, and select Area. Area filtering averages all source pixels corresponding to a destination pixel, making it ideal for downscaling and drastically reducing line shimmer.
  • Use Bicubic or Lanczos: If Area filtering produces an overly soft look, Bicubic (16 samples) or Lanczos (36 samples) provide larger sampling kernels that smooth out high-frequency transitions better than Bilinear.
  • Maintain 1:1 Pixel Mapping: Avoid scaling altogether whenever possible. Setting your source capture to its native resolution prevents interpolation errors entirely.
  • Enable Integer Scaling: If you must downscale, scale by exact integer divisors (such as 2:1) so that pixel boundaries align consistently across every frame.
