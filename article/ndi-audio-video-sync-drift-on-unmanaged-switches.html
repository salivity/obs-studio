NDI Audio Video Sync Drift on Unmanaged Switches

Audio and video synchronization drift when receiving remote NDI streams in OBS Studio across unmanaged network switches is typically caused by a combination of missing traffic prioritization (QoS), hardware clock divergence, packet jitter, and software buffering behaviors. Without managed network controls to handle high-bandwidth NDI data, variable transmission delays affect audio and video packets differently. Over time, these micro-delays accumulate in OBS Studio’s rendering queue, causing the audio to gradually slip out of sync with the video feed.

Lack of Quality of Service (QoS) and Packet Contention

Unmanaged switches treat all network traffic identically using simple FIFO (First-In, First-Out) queuing. NDI streams split audio and video into separate data packets: high-bandwidth, bursty video frames and lightweight, consistent audio chunks.

When network traffic peaks or competes with background data:

  • Unmanaged switches cannot prioritize real-time media packets over general traffic.
  • Video packets, which are substantially larger, frequently get queued or delayed in internal switch buffers (bufferbloat).
  • Audio packets often pass through with less buffering delay due to their small footprint.
  • The uneven delivery of audio versus video creates timing mismatches before the stream ever reaches OBS Studio.

Clock Skew Between Endpoints

Every digital device relies on an internal hardware clock to measure time and frame rates. Because remote senders and the local OBS receiving machine operate on distinct, unsynchronized internal clocks:

  • Even a minute clock difference (e.g., a few parts per million) causes one system to run slightly faster or slower than the other.
  • In professional broadcast networks, Precision Time Protocol (PTP) or Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronizes these clocks over managed switches.
  • Unmanaged switches do not support the precision packet-timing mechanisms required for strict hardware clock synchronization, making clock drift inevitable during extended streaming sessions.

Network Jitter and Packet Loss Recovery

NDI utilizes TCP or UDP (via NDI|HX or RUDP) to transmit streams across local networks. On unmanaged switches lacking Flow Control (IEEE 802.3x):

  • Packet Drops: Transient buffer overruns lead to dropped packets.
  • Retransmission Delays: When TCP retransmits lost packets, it stalls the media pipeline. Audio and video handle this recovery differently; video decoders may skip or freeze frames, while audio streams may either mute or stretch to bridge the gap.
  • Variable Jitter: Inconsistent delivery intervals force the receiving software to constantly guess timestamps, degrading synchronization over time.

OBS Studio Buffer Management and Timestamp Handling

OBS Studio processes media using internal presentation timestamps (PTS). How it handles the NDI stream directly influences drift behavior:

  • Source vs. Network Timing: The NDI source in OBS can synchronize using the sender's embedded timestamps or the local system arrival clock. If set to internal timestamps over an unstable unmanaged link, OBS must dynamically stretch or drop frames to keep up.
  • Asymmetrical Buffering: If the OBS render thread or the video decoding thread experiences even minor frame-processing latency, OBS may drop video frames while continuing to process uninterrupted audio. If OBS does not actively discard corresponding audio samples to compensate, progressive audio lead occurs.

Hardware Bottlenecks on the Receiver

Receiving and decoding multiple remote NDI streams places heavy demands on the host machine's Network Interface Card (NIC) and CPU/GPU decoding pipelines. Unmanaged switches often fail to handle bursty packet flows smoothly, offloading packet pacing to the receiver's NIC. If the NIC driver or CPU encounters interrupt latency:

  • Video decoding threads fall behind the real-time presentation deadline.
  • Audio processing—being computationally lighter—maintains real-time playback.
  • The cumulative delay between the audio render queue and the video render queue manifests as permanent drift until the OBS scene or NDI source is manually refreshed.
