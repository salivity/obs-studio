OBS Studio Output Stalled Warning Explained

The "output stalled" warning in OBS Studio's advanced network diagnostics indicates that OBS has temporarily or completely stopped transmitting stream data because the network connection is blocked, congested, or unresponsive. This diagnostic event means the application's internal network buffer is full, and OBS cannot push new video and audio frames to the streaming platform's ingest server. This guide covers what this warning technically means, the primary causes behind it, and the troubleshooting steps you can take to stabilize your live stream.

What "Output Stalled" Means in OBS Studio

When streaming, OBS Studio continuously encodes audio and video frames and hands them over to a network thread to send via your selected protocol (such as RTMP, RTMPS, or SRT).

Under normal conditions, the streaming destination acknowledges received packets, allowing OBS to clear its send buffer and dispatch the next batch of data. An "output stalled" log entry occurs when the local operating system's network socket refuses to accept more data. This happens because the outbound data queue has reached its capacity, effectively halting output. In the OBS Studio log file, this usually coincides with dropped frames due to network issues and can lead to buffering, hitching, or total stream disconnection for your viewers.

Common Causes of Stalled Output

Several factors can prevent OBS from delivering packets to the streaming server:

  • Insufficient or Inconsistent Upload Bandwidth: If your configured video bitrate is set close to or higher than your actual available upload speed, the connection becomes saturated quickly, causing the queue to back up.
  • Local Network Instability: Streaming over Wi-Fi, using outdated network interface card (NIC) drivers, or sharing a local network with heavy downloads/uploads can introduce high packet loss and latency spikes that stall the output.
  • Hardware Bottlenecks: Malfunctioning routers, deteriorating Ethernet cables, or overloaded network switches can bottleneck traffic before it reaches the internet.
  • ISP Throttling and Routing: Your Internet Service Provider may throttle high-throughput UDP/TCP traffic or route your packets through an unstable path to the platform's ingest server.
  • Server-Side Issues: The streaming platform's server (e.g., Twitch, YouTube, Kick) may be experiencing local congestion, packet drops, or processing delays, preventing it from acknowledging incoming data.

How to Fix the "Output Stalled" Warning

To resolve output stalls and prevent dropped frames, apply the following adjustments:

1. Lower Your Bitrate

Reduce your stream bitrate in Settings > Output. For 1080p streaming, try reducing your bitrate by 1,000 to 2,000 Kbps. Ensure your bitrate does not exceed 75% of your consistent upload speed as determined by independent speed tests.

2. Enable Dynamic Bitrate

In OBS Studio, navigate to Settings > Advanced > Network and enable Dynamically change bitrate to manage congestion (Beta). Instead of stalling the connection and dropping frames when network throughput dips, OBS will automatically lower your bitrate temporarily to keep the stream alive, raising it back up once the connection recovers.

3. Switch to a Wired Connection

Avoid streaming over Wi-Fi. A direct Ethernet cable eliminates radio frequency interference, channel crowding, and packet loss common to wireless connections.

4. Change Streaming Ingest Servers

If the issue is isolated to a specific ingest point, navigate to Settings > Stream and manually select a different regional server instead of relying on the "Auto" option.

5. Inspect Local Network Hardware and Settings

Power-cycle your modem and router to clear corrupted states and caches. In Settings > Advanced > Network, ensure Network Optimizations is enabled, and try setting the Bind to IP option directly to your primary network adapter rather than "Default." Update your network card drivers directly from the manufacturer's website.
