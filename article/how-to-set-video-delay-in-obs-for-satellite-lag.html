How to Set Video Delay in OBS for Satellite Lag

Broadcasting live remote interviews over satellite connections introduces significant transmission latency, often causing noticeable desynchronization between in-studio hosts and remote guests. OBS Studio provides built-in buffering filters and audio offset tools that allow producers to align asynchronous feeds. This guide outlines how to configure custom video and audio delays in OBS Studio to neutralize satellite lag, balance local and remote feeds, and ensure a seamless broadcast.

Understanding Satellite Latency in Broadcasts

Geostationary satellite transmissions travel roughly 44,000 miles round-trip, creating an inherent latency of 250 to 800 milliseconds, which can exceed several seconds when combined with hardware encoding, decoding, and routing.

When conducting a live interview, two synchronization challenges arise:

  1. Internal A/V Desync: The satellite feed's audio and video arrive out of sync with each other.
  2. Local vs. Remote Desync: The local host camera is instantaneous, while the satellite feed is delayed, causing awkward timing during split-screen presentations.

OBS Studio handles both issues using the Video Delay (Async) filter and the Sync Offset audio tool.

Step 1: Compensate for Video Lag (Video Delay Filter)

If you need to delay the local studio camera to match a delayed satellite feed for a split-screen view, apply a video delay filter directly to the local source.

  1. Open OBS Studio and locate the Sources panel.
  2. Right-click your local camera source (or capture card) and select Filters.
  3. In the lower-left corner of the Filters window, click the + icon under Audio/Video Filters.
  4. Select Video Delay (Async) and name the filter (e.g., "Satellite Matching Delay").
  5. Enter the delay duration in milliseconds (ms). For example, enter 1500 for a 1.5-second lag.
  6. Click Close. OBS will now hold video frames in a RAM buffer and output them after the specified interval.

Note: Storing uncompressed video in memory requires substantial RAM. Ensure your system has sufficient memory bandwidth when delaying 1080p or 4K video feeds for several seconds.

Step 2: Compensate for Audio Lag (Sync Offset)

Satellite decoders often process audio faster than heavy video decompression algorithms, leading to audio that leads the video. To realign them:

  1. Locate the Audio Mixer panel in the main OBS interface.
  2. Click the gear icon (or the three vertical dots) next to any audio source and select Advanced Audio Properties.
  3. Locate the audio channel assigned to your satellite receiver.
  4. Adjust the Sync Offset (ms) column:
    • Positive value (e.g., 350 ms): Delays the audio to allow the video feed to catch up.
    • Negative value: Advances the audio if the audio is somehow lagging behind the video (only possible if the video source itself has an active buffer).
  5. Close the window and monitor the feed to verify lip-sync accuracy.

Alternative: Using the Render Delay Filter

If your video source is a direct media stream or browser capture rather than an asynchronous hardware capture device:

  1. Right-click the source and select Filters.
  2. Under Audio/Video Filters, click + and choose Render Delay.
  3. Adjust the slider or input field to set the delay time up to the maximum limit permitted by the filter (typically 500 ms per instance; multiple instances can be stacked if larger delays are required).

Best Practices for Satellite Interviews in OBS

  • Establish a Mix-Minus Audio Setup: While OBS can delay video and audio for the final stream output, the in-ear monitor (IFB) sent back to the satellite guest must never be delayed. Use OBS Studio's monitoring settings or an external mixer to ensure the guest hears the host without artificial latency.
  • Determine Precise Latency: To calculate the exact millisecond offset required, run a visual clapperboard or use an on-screen timecode generator transmitted through the satellite feed, capturing both the sent and received timestamps simultaneously.
  • Monitor RAM Usage: Each second of delayed 1080p60 video requires approximately 500 MB of system memory. Monitor your system's RAM consumption in Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (macOS) to avoid dropped frames during extended broadcasts.
