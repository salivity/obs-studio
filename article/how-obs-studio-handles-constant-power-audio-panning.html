How OBS Studio Handles Constant-Power Audio Panning

This article explains how OBS Studio calculates and applies constant-power panning to maintain consistent perceived loudness across stereo outputs. It breaks down the mathematical sine/cosine panning laws, the handling of mono versus stereo inputs within the OBS audio pipeline, and the acoustic principles that prevent perceived volume drops when moving audio between channels.

The Need for Constant-Power Panning

When moving a sound source across a stereo field, simply adjusting the volume of the left and right channels linearly causes an acoustic phenomenon known as the "center dip." If both channels are set to 50% gain at the center position, the combined acoustic energy drops by approximately 3 dB compared to when the sound is hard-panned to a single side at 100% gain. Conversely, if both channels are kept at 100% in the center, the acoustic energy increases by +3 dB, making centered audio sound significantly louder.

To prevent this variation, digital audio workstations and broadcasting engines like OBS Studio employ a constant-power panning law. This law ensures that the total acoustic energy (\(L^2 + R^2 = 1\)) remains uniform regardless of the pan knob's position.

The Mathematical Model (Sine/Cosine Law)

OBS Studio's underlying audio engine (libobs) utilizes a standard equal-power trigonometric curve, often referred to as the standard 3 dB constant-power law. Instead of linear attenuation, the gain multipliers for the left (\(G_L\)) and right (\(G_R\)) channels are derived using sine and cosine functions.

Given a normalized panning value \(p\) ranging from \(-1.0\) (hard left) to \(+1.0\) (hard right):

  1. Angle Normalization: The pan position is mapped to an angle \(\theta\) across a 90-degree (\(\pi / 2\) radians) quadrant: \[\theta = \frac{\pi}{4} \times (p + 1)\]

  2. Gain Calculation:

    • Left Channel Gain: \(G_L = \cos(\theta)\)
    • Right Channel Gain: \(G_R = \sin(\theta)\)

Practical Application Across Pan Positions:

  • Hard Left (\(p = -1.0\)): \(\theta = 0\) radians. \(G_L = \cos(0) = 1.0\) (0 dB), \(G_R = \sin(0) = 0.0\) (\(-\infty\) dB).
  • Center (\(p = 0.0\)): \(\theta = \pi / 4\) radians (\(45^\circ\)). \(G_L = \cos(\pi / 4) \approx 0.7071\) (\(-3\text{ dB}\)), \(G_R = \sin(\pi / 4) \approx 0.7071\) (\(-3\text{ dB}\)).
  • Hard Right (\(p = 1.0\)): \(\theta = \pi / 2\) radians (\(90^\circ\)). \(G_L = \cos(\pi / 2) = 0.0\) (\(-\infty\) dB), \(G_R = \sin(\pi / 2) = 1.0\) (0 dB).

Because \((0.7071)^2 + (0.7071)^2 = 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.0\), the combined power of both channels at center equals the power of either channel when fully panned, maintaining smooth perceived volume during panning automation or adjustments.

Panning Mono Sources vs. Stereo Balancing

OBS Studio handles mono and stereo inputs differently when routing them to a stereo bus:

  • Mono Inputs: When a source is configured as mono (or downmixed via Advanced Audio Properties), the single incoming audio stream is cloned to both output channels. The constant-power panning algorithm directly modulates these two streams using the calculated \(G_L\) and \(G_R\) coefficients.
  • Stereo Inputs (Balance Control): For stereo sources, applying the panning slider functions primarily as a stereo balance control. In standard operation, rather than collapsing both channels to mono and re-panning them, the engine scales the existing left and right channels using the power-law coefficients to avoid phase issues or altering the existing stereo image. If true panning of a stereo source is required, downmixing to mono first allows the audio engine to treat the track as a single point source within the stereo field.

Processing Within the OBS Audio Pipeline

Audio processing in OBS Studio occurs in real-time floating-point format (typically 32-bit float). Panning calculations are applied during the mixing stage inside libobs:

  1. The raw audio frame is captured from the input device.
  2. Channel layout checks determine whether the input requires conversion, channel mapping, or downmixing.
  3. The panning multipliers (\(G_L, G_R\)) are computed from the user's mixer settings.
  4. The floating-point samples are multiplied by the respective gain coefficients.
  5. The processed channels are summed into the master stereo bus before hitting output limiters and the final streaming/recording encoder.

By applying these constant-power calculations directly within the 32-bit floating-point mixing stage, OBS avoids clipping artifacts, prevents center-channel volume dips, and guarantees that audio positioning remains acoustically transparent to listeners.
