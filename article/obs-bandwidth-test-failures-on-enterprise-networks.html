OBS Bandwidth Test Failures on Enterprise Networks

The OBS Studio Auto-Configuration Wizard often fails to estimate bandwidth on high-speed, symmetric enterprise networks despite the presence of gigabit-class uplink speeds. This article explains the technical reasons behind these test failures, focusing on enterprise security appliances, deep packet inspection, MTU black holes, and aggressive Quality of Service (QoS) policies that disrupt synthetic streaming benchmarks.

How the OBS Bandwidth Test Works

When running the Auto-Configuration Wizard, OBS Studio tests your connection by pushing raw video frames via the Real-Time Messaging Protocol (RTMP) to nearby ingest servers (typically Twitch or YouTube endpoints). The wizard progressively increases the output bitrate, monitoring the TCP socket for packet loss, buffer bloat, and dropped frames to calculate an optimal streaming bitrate.

Unlike standard web-based speed tests that use multi-threaded HTTP/WebSockets over ports 80 and 443, OBS uses continuous single-connection streaming over standard RTMP (port 1935) or RTMPS (port 443).

Deep Packet Inspection and Application Control

Enterprise edge gateways—such as next-generation firewalls (NGFW) from Palo Alto, Fortinet, or Cisco—use Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) to classify application traffic.

  • Protocol Flagging: RTMP traffic on port 1935 is commonly blocked by default in corporate policies to prevent unauthorized data exfiltration or non-work-related media streaming.
  • Heuristic Throttling: When the wizard rapidly ramps up throughput, DPI engines may classify the sudden burst of RTMP chunks as a protocol anomaly or unauthorized video stream, dropping the packets immediately.

SSL/TLS Interception on RTMPS

Modern ingest services increasingly rely on RTMPS over port 443. Enterprise environments frequently deploy SSL/TLS inspection (forward proxy decryption) to monitor encrypted traffic.

When OBS initiates a secure handshake with an ingest server:

  1. The enterprise proxy intercepts the connection and serves a custom internal certificate.
  2. OBS fails to validate the proxy's root certificate against its internal certificate authority store.
  3. The connection terminates instantly, which the wizard reports as a failed bandwidth estimation.

Path MTU Discovery (PMTUD) and Black Holes

Enterprise networks often route traffic through encapsulation protocols such as IPsec, GRE tunnels, or Software-Defined WAN (SD-WAN) overlays. These tunnels reduce the Maximum Transmission Unit (MTU) below the standard 1500 bytes.

  • If the network blocks ICMP "Destination Unreachable / Fragmentation Needed" messages, Path MTU Discovery (PMTUD) fails.
  • As the OBS wizard pushes large TCP segments during its bitrate ramp-up, oversized packets are dropped silently by intermediary routers.
  • The resulting TCP retransmissions exhaust the wizard's timeout thresholds, causing the test to fail.

Aggressive QoS and Traffic Shaping

Corporate networks prioritize business-critical applications (such as VoIP, database syncing, and enterprise SaaS) through DSCP tags and bandwidth allocations:

  • High-bandwidth media streams that lack approved DSCP markings are automatically assigned to "Scavenger" or "Best Effort" classes.
  • Enterprise traffic shapers enforce hard upload ceilings or burst quotas on unclassified streams.
  • When OBS detects an abrupt rate limit or induced latency from traffic policing buffers, the wizard concludes that the connection is unstable and aborts the diagnostic.

Resolving the Issue

To bypass bandwidth estimation failures on enterprise infrastructure:

  • Skip the Wizard: Manually define your target bitrate (e.g., 6,000 to 8,000 Kbps for 1080p60) in Settings > Output.
  • Firewall Exemptions: Request network administrators to allow outbound TCP traffic on port 1935 and bypass SSL decryption for designated streaming ingest domains.
  • Adjust Network Settings: Enable Dynamically change bitrate to manage congestion under Settings > Advanced > Network to let OBS dynamically adapt to enterprise traffic-shaping conditions.
