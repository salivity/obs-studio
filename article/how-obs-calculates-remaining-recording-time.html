How OBS Calculates Remaining Recording Time

OBS Studio estimates your remaining recording time by constantly comparing the available storage space on your target drive against your active output data rate. This real-time calculation translates raw storage capacity into an intuitive hours, minutes, and seconds format, allowing creators to monitor how long their drive can sustain the current recording session before running out of space.

1. Polling Available Disk Space

OBS checks the storage volume associated with your designated output folder. By making periodic low-level operating system calls, OBS determines the exact number of free bytes remaining on that specific drive partition. This check updates regularly to account for files being written in real time—both by OBS itself and by other background processes.

2. Calculating the Total Output Bitrate

To know how quickly storage will be consumed, OBS sums all the active data streams configured for the recording:

  • Video Bitrate: The target data rate set in your output settings (e.g., 6,000 kbps).
  • Audio Bitrates: The combined data rate of all active audio tracks assigned to the recording (e.g., two tracks at 160 kbps each equals 320 kbps).

These streams are added together to find the combined bandwidth requirement. For instance, a 6,000 kbps video stream paired with 320 kbps of audio yields a total rate of 6,320 kbps.

3. Converting Bits to Bytes

Storage capacity is measured in bytes, while broadcast and encoding bitrates are configured in bits. OBS converts the combined bitrate from kilobits or megabits per second into bytes per second using standard data conversion:

\[\text{Bytes Per Second} = \frac{\text{Total Bitrate in Bits Per Second}}{8}\]

Using the 6,320 kbps example:

  1. \(6,320 \text{ kbps} \times 1,000 = 6,320,000 \text{ bits per second}\)
  2. \(6,320,000 / 8 = 790,000 \text{ bytes (or 790 KB) per second}\)

OBS also applies a minor buffer multiplier to account for container muxing overhead (such as MP4, MKV, or MOV container metadata), ensuring the estimate does not dangerously underestimate disk usage.

4. The Core Calculation

Once free storage and data consumption rates are established in matching units, OBS calculates the remaining duration in seconds:

\[\text{Remaining Time (seconds)} = \frac{\text{Available Disk Space (bytes)}}{\text{Output Rate (bytes per second)}}\]

If a drive has 50 GB (approximately 50,000,000,000 bytes) free and the recording consumes 790,000 bytes per second:

\[50,000,000,000 / 790,000 \approx 63,291 \text{ seconds}\]

OBS converts these seconds into standard digital clock notation (HH:MM:SS), displaying roughly 17 hours, 34 minutes, and 51 seconds of remaining time in the status bar.

5. Constant Bitrate (CBR) vs. Variable Rate Controls (CQP/CRF/VBR)

  • Under CBR (Constant Bitrate): The calculation remains stable and accurate because the data rate remains consistent regardless of scene complexity.
  • Under Rate Controls like CQP, CRF, or VBR: File size changes depending on movement, lighting, and detail in the frame. Because the true future bitrate is unpredictable, OBS bases its estimate on a rolling average of recently encoded frames or nominal target settings. Consequently, the remaining time counter may fluctuate dynamically during high-motion scenes.
