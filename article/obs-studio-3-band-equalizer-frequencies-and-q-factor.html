OBS Studio 3-Band Equalizer Frequencies and Q Factor

The 3-band equalizer filter in OBS Studio is a simplified audio tool designed to adjust tone across low, middle, and high frequency ranges without requiring complex parametric configuration. Under the hood, OBS defines these frequency ranges and their resonance through hardcoded biquad filter structures rather than dynamic crossover networks. This article details how the filter sets its crossover points, calculates its Q-factor values, and applies standard IIR digital filtering across its three frequency bands.

Filter Architecture and Crossover Frequencies

Unlike dynamic crossover filters that split the incoming audio stream into separate frequency bands using crossover networks (such as Linkwitz-Riley filters), the OBS Studio 3-band equalizer employs three cascaded second-order IIR (infinite impulse response) biquad filters in series:

  1. Low Band: Implemented as a second-order low-shelf filter. Its transition cutoff frequency is fixed around 300 Hz. Signals below this point are boosted or cut based on the user's slider value, while frequencies significantly above it pass through unmodified.
  2. Mid Band: Implemented as a second-order peaking (bell) filter. It is centered at 1,000 Hz (1 kHz) to cover the primary vocal and presence frequencies.
  3. High Band: Implemented as a second-order high-shelf filter. Its corner frequency is fixed around 3,200 Hz to 4,000 Hz, applying gain adjustments to higher harmonics, sibilance, and air.

Because the equalizer operates via serial shelving and peaking stages, the "crossover" points are not sharp division lines. Instead, they are acoustic crossover zones where the roll-off of the low-shelf filter smoothly overlaps with the skirt of the mid-peaking filter, and the upper skirt of the mid-peaking filter overlaps with the onset of the high-shelf filter.

Q-Factor Definitions

The Quality factor (Q) in OBS Studio’s equalizer is internally static and not exposed in the user interface. It governs the sharpness, bandwidth, and resonance around the specified frequencies:

  • Shelving Filters (Low and High): The low-shelf and high-shelf filters are designed with a standard Butterworth Q-factor of approximately \(0.707\) (\(1 / \sqrt{2}\)). This value ensures a maximally flat response with no resonant peaks or dips near the cutoff frequencies when the filter gain is applied, preventing unwanted frequency hyping at the boundary points.
  • Mid-Band Peaking Filter: The mid-band peaking filter utilizes a moderate Q-factor set close to \(1.0\) (corresponding to a bandwidth of roughly 1.4 octaves). This broad bandwidth ensures a smooth transition between the low and high shelving stages, minimizing phase cancellation and steep notches across the midrange.

Operational Characteristics

When all sliders (Low, Mid, High) are set to 0.0 dB, the biquad equations evaluate to unity gain, resulting in a completely flat frequency response with transparent audio throughput. Because the Q-factors and center frequencies are fixed, the filter behaves like a classic broad-band DJ mixer EQ. For tasks requiring steep crossovers, adjustable bandwidths, or surgical frequency correction, users typically integrate specialized VST 2.x plugins into OBS Studio rather than using the built-in 3-band filter.
